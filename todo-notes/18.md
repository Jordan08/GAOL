# Point 18: reading a long number under a comma locale is slow

Report of the agent that made the change (written in English, as produced), and the reviews. The branch is `todo-18-reader-locale-speed`; head at the time: `0e52b33`.

## Commits

- 1b043c7 Numbers are taken apart once, not for each comparison with a double: long numbers read fast under a comma locale
- 0e52b33 Document the checks of long numbers under a comma locale in doc/tests.md

## What was wrong and what the change does

gaol_enclose_number() compares the number with the doubles about it (two comparisons when strtod() is right, up to about 125 when it is far off, as under a comma locale where strtod("1.5...") stops at the point). Each call of gaol_compare_number(const char*, double) parsed the whole text again: digits into a big natural (one pass per digit, quadratic), then N*5^k or M*5^-k by repeated multiplications. Reproduced on the base: a 20000-digit number read in 2.3 to 6 s under fr_FR.utf8 against 0.04 to 0.2 s under the C locale (ratios 29 to 53). I chose the first option of the point, parsing once, and made it stronger: gaol_take_apart() reads the text once into a gaol_number (zero flag, "beyond the doubles" flag, k+k2, N*5^k for k>=0, or N and 5^-k for k<0), and gaol_compare_number(const gaol_number&, double) is left with one product (5^-k by the 53-bit M, new gaol_natural_mul_u64), one shift and one comparison, linear in the length of the text. The result cannot depend on the locale nor on strtod(), which only starts the search: the outcome is the unique lo with v >= lo and v < next(lo), whatever the guess; the comparison itself is unchanged in meaning. A 20000-digit number now reads in about 0.02 to 0.05 s under both locales (ratio 1.0 to 1.2); 100000 digits take 0.55 s (the single parse of the digits stays quadratic, as under the C locale before). Short numbers are not slower (0.1: about 550 ns against 945 ns, machine noisy). I did not choose the second option (a copy of the text with localeconv()'s decimal separator): it would leave the quadratic re-parse in every comparison whenever strtod() is off (other libraries, multibyte separators) and localeconv() returns static storage that is not thread-safe. The lexer was regenerated with cmake --target parser (a forced regeneration gives no diff).

## Tests

tests/numbers.cpp: new long_numbers()/check_long_numbers()/long_numbers_c_locale() and the extension of comma_locale(). 12 long numbers of 5000 digits whose tightest enclosure is known without the reader (1.5 + zeros = 1.5, + zeros + 1 = [1.5, next], 1.999... = [prev(2), 2], 1<zeros>e-n = 1, 15<zeros>e-(n+1) = 1.5, 0.<zeros>1en = [prev(0.1), 0.1], hexadecimal 0x1.<zeros>8p0 and 0x1.8<zeros>p1, above and below the doubles, uncertain forms 1.5<zeros>? and ?1), plus the 20000-character 1.5<zeros>1, under the C locale and under fr_FR (skipped where no comma locale); and under the comma locale, the least of 3 readings of the 20000-character number must be at most 10 times the least of 3 under the C locale plus 50 ms (steady_clock, same process, so a slow machine scales both). The existing TIMEOUT 300 of the test is kept. No new test file, so CMakeLists/Makefile.am/meson.build are unchanged.

## Behaviour change

No change of result: the enclosures are identical (differential test below). Reading a long number (thousands of characters) under a locale writing a decimal comma, or with a strtod() far from the number, takes the time of the C locale (20000 characters: 2.3-6 s down to about 0.03 s) instead of a time quadratic in the length times about 125 comparisons; the C locale and short numbers are as fast or faster.

## Risks

Low. The comparison logic is the same code moved (parsing into gaol_take_apart, per-double part in gaol_compare_number), with one new function, gaol_natural_mul_u64 (all indices within n.size()+2, the carry bound 2^64-1 is commented, leading zeros popped), covered by the differential test and by ASan/UBSan. gaol_take_apart() now runs before round_nearest(), so an exception there (bad_alloc) no longer leaves the rounding mode changed. gaol_number holds three vectors, returned by value (C++11 move, and at worst one copy of a text-sized natural, once per reading). Platforms not testable here (macOS, MSVC, MinGW, s390x big-endian, riscv64...): the code has no endianness, floating-point or locale dependence (bits via memcpy as before, integers only). The new test adds about 1 s of CPU natively (about 30 readings of 20000 or 5000 characters); under qemu (s390x, ppc64le, riscv64) it scales with the machine but the numbers test keeps its 300 s TIMEOUT (ctest) and timeout 300 (meson); if the qemu jobs are already close to it, the 5000/20000 digit counts can be lowered (5000 would still fail without the fix by a factor of about 40, but with a narrower margin because of the constant 50 ms).

## Open questions and decisions for the maintainer

1) The single parse of the digits is still quadratic in the number of digits (one multiply-add pass per digit): 20000 digits 0.02 s, 100000 digits 0.55 s, under every locale, as it was under the C locale. Taking 9 decimal digits (7 hexadecimal) per pass would make it about 9 times faster, and a divide-and-conquer conversion would make it subquadratic; not done, since the point is about the repetition of the parse. To decide if reading numbers of 10^5 digits matters. 2) The second option of the point (giving strtod() a copy of the text with localeconv()'s separator) was not taken, for the reasons in the summary; with the parse done once, the roughly 105 comparisons under a comma locale cost about 10 microseconds each, so it would bring nothing measurable. 3) gaol_read_uncertain() builds its sums with std::string::insert at the front (gaol_decimal_add_sub), quadratic in the length too: a 20000-digit uncertain number takes about 0.07 s against 0.045 s for a plain one, so it is not a problem now, but 100000 digits would be slower than the plain form. 4) The first read of a text in a process is slower (0.2 s cold against 0.045 s warm for 20000 digits in my first measurements): the test takes the least of 3 readings for that reason. 5) The 20000-digit reading through the uncertain form, hexadecimal and exponent forms was checked only under GCC/clang on x86-64 here.

## Reviews

### 18-correctness-0: approve

Non-blocking remarks:
- Commit 1b043c7's subject line is 113 characters; the house rules say under about 100 where possible. Rewording the unpushed subject would fix it.
- gaol/gaol_interval_lexer.lpp:216, the comment on gaol_number::beyond, reads as if beyond were set whenever v is below the least positive double or above the largest. The code sets it only when v is far beyond (10^311 and up, or below 10^-330), and the exact comparison handles the rest. Suggest: 'far above the largest double, or far below the least positive one'. The same line is over 100 columns.
- The top comment of gaol_enclose_number says the time of a reading under a comma locale 'is that of a reading under the C locale'. I measured a ratio of 1.0 to 1.2 (about 100 more cheap comparisons), so 'about the same' would be exact.
- tests/numbers.cpp:995: the failure message is built with std::to_string while the comma locale is still set, so it prints '2,112549 s'. It is cosmetic. Building the message before the setlocale calls, or using a stream imbued with the C locale, would give a dot.
- Open questions 1 and 3 of the report are confirmed and not blocking: the digit parse and gaol_decimal_add_sub are still quadratic. The uncertain form at 100000 digits took 1.3 s in my run against 0.5 s for the plain form.
- The new numbers test costs about 1 s of CPU natively (1.9 s in total). Under qemu jobs (s390x, ppc64le, riscv64) it scales with the machine, but the 300 s TIMEOUT is far away. The 50 ms slack of the timing check is absolute and therefore safe on slow machines.
