# Point 1: the pow of the standard is written twice

Report of the agent that made the change (written in English, as produced), and the reviews. The branch is `todo-01-pow-norm-half`; head at the time: `23bbb50`.

## Commits

- 38f41a4 The pow of Table 9.1 is written once, pow_standard(), which gaol_pow_hybrid() and gaol_ieee1788::pow call
- 23bbb50 doc/tests.md: the test of the bounds of pow on 89 boxes

## What was wrong and what the change does

gaol_ieee1788::pow made its own checks (x cut to [0,+oo], the empty sets, x = {0}, the beyond-the-ints block) and then called gaol_pow_hybrid(), which made them again, so the pow of Table 9.1 existed in two copies. Its second half (empty sets, cut of x, 0^y, degenerate integer exponent, CORE-MATH corners, exp(y log x)) is now the file-local function pow_standard(x, y) of gaol/gaol_interval.cpp (static, no new exported symbol). gaol_pow_hybrid() keeps its checks of the empty sets and of [+oo]/[-oo], adds only the pown case of a degenerate integer exponent (pown on the whole of x, [-oo,+oo] beyond the ints) and calls pow_standard() for every other exponent; gaol_ieee1788::pow is one call to pow_standard(). Deviation from the letter of the TODO: the beyond-the-ints block of gaol_ieee1788::pow and the within-ints pown case on x cut to [0,+oo] moved into pow_standard() too (gaol_pow_hybrid() never reaches them, it takes [n] first), because keeping them in gaol_ieee1788::pow would need the cut of x and the x = {0} case there again, which is the duplication the point removes. The proof that nothing changed is a differential run of one program linked with the base library (a2ca992) and the new one: gaol_ieee1788::pow, gaol::pow(x, y) and gaol::pow(x, p), hex bounds, on 10 500 grid boxes under the four rounding directions plus 60 000 random boxes; the FPU build is bit for bit identical, the SSE2 build too except for one sign of zero (see behaviour_change). A compact version (89 boxes, expected values as hex literals taken from the base library, all checked against mpmath at 500 bits: enclosing, within one double of the tightest bounds) is in tests/ieee1788.cpp, and it passes on the base library and on the new one. No bug was fixed, so the test is a characterization test; its teeth were shown by 29 deliberate mutations of the refactored functions (24 killed, the 5 survivors are equivalent mutants).

## Tests

tests/ieee1788.cpp, new function pow_on_boxes() called from main(): 89 boxes {x, y, expected gaol_ieee1788::pow, expected gaol::pow when different}, each reaching a branch of pow_standard() (empty base or exponent, x < 0, x cut to {0}, [0] with y <= 0 and y > 0, degenerate integer exponents within the ints for x >= 0 and for a negative part, beyond the ints incl. limits 2^31 and 2^31+1, lower bound 0 / -0 / 1, infinite bounds, the corners for a base from 0, above 1, below 1, around 1, and exp(y log x) for an infinite bound or a base from 0 with y <= 0) or one of the two additions of gaol::pow (pown on the whole of x, [-oo,+oo] beyond the ints). Each box is checked on gaol_ieee1788::pow(x, y), gaol::pow(x, y) and, for a degenerate y = [p], gaol_ieee1788::pow(x, p) and gaol::pow(x, p) (306 checks). Bounds compared with == (the sign of a zero is not tested, so the test does not depend on interval::zero() vs interval(0.0) nor on the platform). Expected values: hex literals from the base library a2ca992, identical in the four builds base/new x SSE2/FPU, and verified apart from GAOL against the exact power computed with mpmath at 500 bits (script checkrows.py in the scratch space): all 89 enclose it, largest distance to the tightest bound 1 double.

## How the test was shown to fail without the fix

No bug, so the proof is by mutation of the refactored code with the final tests/ieee1788.cpp (script $SCR/tmp/01-pow-norm-half/run_mutations.py, which recompiles gaol/gaol_interval.cpp with one edit, replaces it in a copy of libgaol.a of the sse build and runs the test): 24 of 29 mutants make the test fail on the new boxes, e.g. x not cut to [0,+oo] (24 boxes fail), no x = {0} case (6), 0^y for y >= 0 (4), degenerate integer within the ints taken by the corners instead of pown (6), pown on the whole of x in pow_standard (8), beyond-the-ints block dropped (8), its lower bound not clamped / no +0 / wrong monotony / no special case for 1 (10 / 2 / 6 / 4), gaol_pow_hybrid() without pown (10) or with pow_standard() beyond the ints instead of [-oo,+oo] (36), pown on the positive part in gaol_pow_hybrid (9), gaol_ieee1788::pow calling gaol_pow_hybrid (56), the corners dropped (38) or each corner choice altered (2 to 8 boxes each), corners with an infinite x (4) or for a base from 0 with y <= 0 (10). The 5 survivors are equivalent: y empty unchecked in pow_standard (NaN bounds still give the empty set), exp(y*log(x)) instead of log(base) (log cuts to its domain), the guard of gaol_pow_hybrid for [+oo]/[-oo] removed (unreachable: interval(oo) is the empty set), the special case at_upper == 1 (CORE-MATH's pow(1, n) is exactly 1), and a redundant is_empty in gaol_ieee1788::pow. The unmodified test also passes with the base library (376 checks, 0 failed: it characterizes the behaviour before the change) and with the new one.

## Behaviour change

No bound changes, only one sign of zero. gaol::pow(x, y) and gaol::pow(x, p), when x cut to [0,+oo] is {0} (x = [0], [-1, 0], ...) and y is not a degenerate integer with y.right() > 0, returned [-0, +0] with SSE2 intervals (interval::zero() has the stored lower bound +0, so left() is -0, printed "<-0, 0>"); it is now [+0, +0] (printed "<0, 0>"), which gaol_ieee1788::pow gave in both builds and gaol::pow in the FPU build. The two functions returned different bits for the same input in the SSE2 build; sharing one function forces one choice, and interval(0.0) was taken over interval::zero() so that the standard's pow keeps its output. -0 == +0, so no comparison changes. Differential results: FPU build 70 500/70 500 lines identical; SSE2 build: gaol_ieee1788::pow identical, gaol::pow differs only in 5 126 zero lower bounds of a [0,0] result (-0 to +0), nothing else; GAOL_PRESERVE_ROUNDING build (SSE2) the same as the SSE2 build; no result depends on the rounding direction in effect at the call. Timing (SSE2, GCC 9.4, i7-1185G7, best of 7, alternated 6 times): gaol::pow with a non-integer exponent +2 to +3 % (about 2-3 ns of 100 ns: one more call and one repeated check), gaol_ieee1788::pow with a non-integer exponent -3 %, with the integer exponent [3] -6 to -11 %, gaol::pow(x,[3]) -6 %, gaol::pow(x, 3) unchanged.

## Risks

Small slowdown of gaol::pow with a non-integer exponent (+2 to +3 %, 2-3 ns), measured with GCC 9.4 only (not with clang-18); the integer paths are faster. The expected values of the 89 boxes are bit-for-bit hex literals, computed by CORE-MATH (correctly rounded in the direction in effect), GAOL's pown and, for the exp(y log x) rows, the composition of CORE-MATH exp and log with the interval product: deterministic on IEEE platforms, the same in the four builds tried here, but not run on the CI platforms that are not available here (32-bit x87, big-endian, MSVC, arm64...); if one of them rounds the composition differently, only those rows would fail. The sign of zero of gaol::pow([0], y>0) changed with SSE2 intervals (see behaviour_change); no test in the tree depended on it. The deviation from the wording of the TODO (beyond-ints block moved into pow_standard()) is a design choice, see open_questions.

## Open questions and decisions for the maintainer

1. Design choice for the maintainer: the TODO says gaol_ieee1788::pow calls the "standard" half directly except for its integer exponents beyond the ints. I put the beyond-ints block (and the within-ints pown on x cut to [0,+oo]) inside pow_standard() so that the cut of x, the empty sets and x = {0} exist once; gaol_pow_hybrid() takes [n] before reaching those two branches. The alternative (block kept in gaol_ieee1788::pow) needs xp and the x = {0} case there again. 2. Sign of zero: I took interval(0.0) rather than interval::zero() in the x = {0} case, so gaol::pow([0], y>0) prints <0, 0> instead of <-0, 0> with SSE2 intervals; keeping interval::zero() would instead make gaol_ieee1788::pow print <-0, 0>. Say if the other choice is wanted. 3. Noticed, not changed: the guard of gaol_pow_hybrid() for a degenerate exponent [+oo] or [-oo] is unreachable (interval(oo) and interval(-oo) are the empty set, so such an exponent is caught by is_empty() first); and the special case at_upper == 1.0 of the beyond-ints branch is redundant (CORE-MATH's pow(1, n) is exactly 1). 4. Noticed, not changed: pown at high powers is not bit-identical between the SSE2 and FPU builds (e.g. pown([-2, 3], 100): upper bound 0x1.69194f299cdddp+158 with FPU, 0x1.69194f299cddbp+158 with SSE2, both enclosures of 3^100, the SSE2 one tighter; 1 762 lines of the differential output differ between base-sse and base-fpu, the same before and after this change), which doc/differences.md's "same bounds bit for bit in the SSE2 and FPU builds" does not claim for pown. 5. The mutation and differential scripts are in the scratch space ($SCR/tmp/01-pow-norm-half: diffpow.cpp, run_mutations.py, mutate2.py, checkrows.py, gen_table.py, final/), not in the tree.

## Reviews

### 1-correctness-0: approve

Non-blocking remarks:
- doc/tests.md (new lines on the ieee1788 test) and tests/ieee1788.cpp (header comment line 17, comment of pow_on_boxes line 134) say the bounds are checked 'bit for bit'. same() compares with ==, so a -0 and a +0 both pass, and gaol::pow([0], y>0) did change its sign of zero. Suggest 'bound for bound (a -0 and a +0 are the same bound)'. The 'when each had its own copy of the pow' wording in doc/tests.md is history that will read oddly once merged; say what the test checks (the hex values, enclosure within one double of the tightest bounds).
- gaol_pow_hybrid() keeps its own is_empty() check and the [+oo]/[-oo] guard. Both are now redundant with pow_standard() and gaol_pown(): the mutant without the is_empty() check survives, and the author reports the guard unreachable. Dropping them would make 'checked once' complete for gaol::pow and recover part of the reported +2-3 % on non-integer exponents. Optional, and defensible to leave for the maintainer.
- The header comment of pow_standard() and the sentence 'where the corners below are one double wide' will need rewording after TODO point 3, as the author notes. The gaol_pow_hybrid() Doxygen in gaol/gaol_interval.h still says 'exp(J log(I))' for the standard part, whereas the corners of CORE-MATH's pow are used for finite bounds. This was already so before the change and is not made worse by it.
- Pre-existing, sound, not caused by this change (seen in my runs): pown for |n| near 2^31 is very loose (e.g. [0x1.fffffffffffd8p-1, 1.8e300]^(-2^31) gives an upper bound about 1e-6 relative above the exact one), which is TODO point 8. The exp(y log x) path with an infinite bound can be hundreds of doubles wide (e.g. [-oo, 2^-1000]^[-oo,-1], lower bound 617 doubles below 2^1000). pown at high powers differs between SSE2 and FPU, as the author says.
