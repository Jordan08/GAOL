## What was wrong and what the change does

No change was made. The investigation found that the reason the refusal gives
is wrong, and that the real reasons are still there after point 6.

**The stated reason is wrong.** `gaol/gaol_config.h`, the docs and the CI say
that mingw-w64 before 12 "answers `fegetround()` from a state of its own". The
sources of mingw-w64 v9.0.0, v10.0.0, v11.0.1 and v12.0.0 have the same
`misc/fegetround.c`, `misc/fesetround.c` and `misc/fesetenv.c`, byte for byte.
The same holds for `math/fma.c` and `math/round.c` (tarballs from
sourceforge, `tmp/06b/src`). Disassembly of `libmingwex.a` of the mingw-w64 11
installed here shows:

- `fegetround()` is `fnstcw` masked with 0xc00. It reads the x87 control word,
  as glibc does, with no state of its own.
- `fesetround()` writes the x87 control word, and MXCSR when `cpuid` reports
  SSE.
- `fesetenv(FE_DFL_ENV)` calls `_fpreset`, which is `fninit` and touches only
  the x87 unit.

So `fegetround()` cannot be what separates mingw-w64 11 (refused) from
mingw-w64 12 (accepted). It first changes in v13, which reads it through
`__mingw_controlfp`. Point 6 still helps on MinGW x64. The x87-only
`fegetround()` is also in the mingw-w64 12 of the accepted MinGW-Builds 14.2.0
(rt_v12), and experiment (f) below shows that point 6's fix is needed there.
It is not the reason for the refusal.

**The real difference is `fma()` and `round()`.** In mingw-w64 12,
`math/fma.c`, `math/round.c` and some siblings moved in
`mingw-w64-crt/Makefile.am`. Before, they were in `src_libmingwex`, which is
linked into every program, and before the C runtime (the GCC spec is
`-lmingwex -lmsvcrt`). Now they are in `src_msvcrt_common`, which only
`libmsvcrt.a` carries. A UCRT toolchain of mingw-w64 12 or later therefore
takes `fma` and `round` from `ucrtbase.dll`, and a toolchain before 12 takes
them from libmingwex, whatever its C runtime. The Chocolatey packages of the CI
are all UCRT:

| Package | Build | Runtime |
|---|---|---|
| 12.2.0.03042023 | MinGW-Builds | ucrt-rt_v10 |
| 13.2.0 | MinGW-Builds | ucrt-rt_v11 |
| 14.2.0 | MinGW-Builds | ucrt-rt_v12 |
| 15.2.0 | MinGW-Builds | ucrt-rt_v13 |
| 11.2.0.07112021 | WinLibs | mingw-w64 9.0.0 |

So GCC 11 to 13 on x64 get the mingw-w64 functions, and GCC 14 and 15 get the
UCRT ones. That is the split the CI saw.

- **`fma()` of mingw-w64 is not correctly rounded.** It splits x and y at 27
  bits (Dekker), then computes `z + xhi*yhi + (xhi*ylo + xlo*yhi) + xlo*ylo`
  with four roundings. The x86-64 build does this with SSE doubles.
  `tmp/06b/fmatest.c` ran 200,000 pseudo-random triples in the four directions
  and compared it with the hardware FMA (glibc's `fma` on this Xeon). 283,692
  of the 800,000 results differ:
  - For generic triples: 24,754 of 100,000 differ to nearest, 73,165 upward,
    73,176 downward and 69,621 toward zero.
  - For `fma(a, b, -a*b)`, the error-free product that double-double
    arithmetic is built on: 10,744 of 100,000 differ in each direction.

  GAOL gives GCC for Windows no `-mfma` (CMakeLists.txt, AVX stack
  alignment). So `__builtin_fma` in CORE-MATH (29 calls in tan.o, 26 in
  pow.o) and `std::fma` in `gaol/gaol_interval.cpp` (32 calls: GAOL's exact
  products for integer powers and the reduction of sin and cos) are all calls
  to this function. Example: for tiny x, `cr_tan` returns
  `__builtin_fma(x, 0x1p-54, x)`. Rounded upward, the naive fma adds the two
  halves of `x*2^-54` separately and rounds up twice, giving `next(next(x))`.
  GAOL's lower bound `previous_float(cr_tan(x))` is then `next(x)`, above
  tan(x). That is the "tan, asin, atan of small arguments" the CI saw.
- **`round()` of mingw-w64 depends on the rounding direction.** It is
  `res = ceil(x); if (res - x > 0.5) res -= 1`. For x = 0x1.fffffffffffffp-2,
  `1 - x` is exactly 0.5 + 2^-54, which rounds to 0.5 to nearest. So
  `round(x)` is 1 to nearest, downward and toward zero, and 0 only upward. The
  exact roundTiesToAway is 0. `round_ties_to_away()` (gaol_interval.h) calls
  `std::round` in the caller's direction, so it returns [1, 1] after the
  program sets the direction to nearest. CORE-MATH's pow.o also calls `round`
  (3 calls), always in GAOL's upward direction, where this `round` is right.

## Tests

The cross build uses the mingw-w64 11 of Ubuntu (`x86_64-w64-mingw32-g++-posix`,
GCC 13, `__MINGW64_VERSION_MAJOR` 11, msvcrt), wine 9.0 with a private prefix
(`tmp/06b/wine`), and the toolchain file `tmp/06b/mingw64-toolchain.cmake`
(`CMAKE_SYSTEM_NAME Windows`, `-static`, `CMAKE_CROSSCOMPILING_EMULATOR
wine`). It is Release with `WITH_TESTS=ON`, 31 ctest tests (intervalf and
interval2f are skipped), run through gcore at -j1 with `WINEDEBUG=-all`. The
refusal was disabled only in exported copies (`git archive` into `tmp/06b/src-*`,
with `< 12` changed to `< 11` for x86-64). Nothing of this was committed.

- **(a) origin/configure-clean (0f2963c), refusal disabled:** 3 tests fail
  (`ctest-a.log`):
  - `elementary`: 29 of 367,505 checks. They are "encloses" failures of tan,
    asin, atan, sinh, cosh, atanh, atan2 and sin([-1e-300, 1e-300]) at small
    arguments, e.g. `tan(0x1.56e1fc2f8f359p-997) = [0x1.56e1fc2f8f35ap-997,
    ...]`, and 2 checks of "the integer functions" at ±0x1.fffffffffffffp-2.
  - `reverse`: 4 of 4,089 (`asin_rel`, `atan_rel`).
  - `core_math`: 136,591 of 1,472,070. About 2,250 of 9,225 per function for
    sin, cos, tan, asin, atan, sinh, cosh, tanh, asinh, atanh, expm1 and log1p;
    68,597 for hypot.

  This is the evidence the refusal was needed, and it matches what the CI saw.
- **(b) todo-06-fegetround-mxcsr (a1ddacb), refusal disabled:** the same 3
  tests fail with exactly the same 138 FAILED lines and the same counts
  (`ctest-b.log`). Point 6 changes nothing here. `rounding_direction` passes,
  16,953 checks, the pow-with-subnormal checks of point 6 included
  (`rounding_direction-b.log`).
- **(c) as (b), with a correct fma linked in:** `tmp/06b/fma_hw.o` is
  `vfmadd132sd`, and it gives the same 800,000 results as glibc under wine. It
  is linked before libmingwex, so it replaces libmingwex's `fma` in every
  program. Only the 2 checks of "the integer functions" remain (the `round()`
  above); every other test passes (`ctest-c.log`).
- **(d) as (a), with the same correct fma:** the same single residue
  (`ctest-d.log`). So on configure-clean too, everything that failed except
  `round` came from `fma`, not from `fegetround()`.
- **(e) as (b), with the `fma` and `round` objects of MSYS2's MINGW64 linked
  in:** these come from `lib64_libmsvcrt_common_a-{fma,round}.o` of
  `mingw-w64-x86_64-crt-git-13.0.0.r91` from repo.msys2.org, that is
  mingw-w64 13 with msvcrt, a configuration GAOL accepts today. The result is
  the same 138 FAILED lines as (b) (`ctest-e.log`). That `fma` alone also
  gives results bit-identical to mingw-w64 11's on the 800,000 triples.
- **(f) point 6's test without point 6's fix, correct fma:** only
  `gaol/core_math_port.h` of configure-clean was put back, and
  `rounding_direction` was built and run. It fails 9 checks: 6 of "pow with a
  subnormal result: the same result whatever the rounding direction" and 3 of
  "the tightest enclosure", all in the state "to nearest, and upward for SSE"
  (`rounding_direction-f.log`). These are the 9 failures point 6 found with
  glibc. With the fix, (b) and (c), they pass.
- 32-bit x86 was not tested: wine32 is not installed. The i686 libmingwex
  `fma` computes the same algorithm on the x87 unit. A Linux emulation with
  `long double` (`fmatest-x87.c`) gives 0 errors for `fma(a, b, -a*b)` and
  22 to 41 wrong of 100,000 generic triples per direction. The i686 `round`
  is exact there thanks to the extended precision.

## Local validation

- `fpstate.c` under wine: the x87 control word is 0x37f and MXCSR 0x1f80 in a
  constructor and in `main`. `fegetround()` still returns 0 with only MXCSR
  set upward. `fesetround(FE_DOWNWARD)` gives cw 0x77f and MXCSR 0x3f80.
  `fesetenv(FE_DFL_ENV)` gives cw 0x37f with MXCSR left at 0x3f80.
- Wine against Windows: the functions involved (`fma`, `round`,
  `fegetround`, `fesetround`, `fesetenv`, `_fpreset`) come from libmingwex and
  libmingw32. They are linked into the .exe and run natively. Wine gives only
  msvcrt.dll (printf, strtod, ceil, all exact or not involved). The startup
  state is `fninit` of mingw-w64's `_fpreset` (x87 0x37f, 64-bit precision,
  nearest) and MXCSR 0x1f80, as on Windows. I know of no difference that
  matters, and the symptoms of (a) are the ones the CI reported on Windows.
- No Linux build was run: the branch has no change over its base.
- The manual was not compiled: it was not edited.

## Open questions and decisions for the maintainer

1. **Soundness hole: mingw-w64 12 and later linked with msvcrt.dll are
   accepted but get the same `fma()` and `round()`.** Examples are MSYS2's
   MINGW64 environment, the msvcrt builds of MinGW-Builds, and the default
   mingw-w64 cross compilers of Debian, Ubuntu and Fedora, whose
   `__MSVCRT_VERSION__` is 0x700. `gaol_config.h` accepts them on x86-64
   (`__MINGW64_VERSION_MAJOR >= 12`), and experiment (e) shows the same wrong
   bounds with MSYS2's own objects. The CI never sees them: its MinGW and MSYS2
   jobs are all UCRT. I did not fix it, because the task was only to lift or
   keep the refusal. There are two options:
   - (a) Refuse on x86-64 every mingw-w64 whose `fma` comes from mingw-w64,
     and correct the reason given. For example:
     `defined(__MINGW64_VERSION_MAJOR) && ((defined(__x86_64__) &&
     (__MINGW64_VERSION_MAJOR < 12 || !defined(_UCRT))) ||
     (!defined(__x86_64__) && __MINGW64_VERSION_MAJOR < 11))`.
     The message would name the `fma()` and `round()` of mingw-w64's own math
     library. The texts to correct are the comment and #error of
     `gaol/gaol_config.h`, CMakeLists.txt around line 203, configure.ac
     around 231, meson.build line 73 (which already says "no longer refused"),
     `doc/three-builds.md` (table row and last paragraph),
     `doc/continuous-integration.md` (lines 43 and 82), `manual/v5/gaol.tex`
     (276 and 331), the comments of `.github/workflows/windows.yml`,
     `doc/differences.md` (ledger), and possibly a refused MSYS2 MINGW64 job.
     I recommend this option: it is the smallest change and fits "soundness
     first".
   - (b) Give CORE-MATH and GAOL a correctly rounded `fma` and a
     direction-free `round` where the C library's are mingw-w64's, and then
     accept those toolchains. This is more code; a software fma is slower.
     `-mfma` for the CORE-MATH sources alone is not an option, because of the
     AVX stack alignment of GCC for Windows.
2. **32-bit x86:** the refusal before 11 stays, since point 6 is x86-64 only.
   Its stated reason, the "state of its own", does not match the sources
   either: the i686 `fegetround` of v9 to v12 is the same `fnstcw`. The
   comment of `GAOL_RND_MINGW_FENV_ONLY` in `gaol/gaol_fpu_fenv.h` says the
   same thing. The i686 `fma` is computed on the x87 unit: my emulation found
   it exact for error-free products but wrong in about 0.03 % of generic
   triples. The CI passes 12.2 and 13.2 x86, but that `fma` is not correctly
   rounded. This is untested here (no wine32).
3. A run-time check that the `fma()` GAOL calls is correctly rounded (a few
   known triples in `tests/core_math.cpp`) would catch such a C library on any
   platform the CI adds. This is not done.
