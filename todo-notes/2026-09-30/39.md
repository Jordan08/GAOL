## What was wrong and what the change does

This commit applies the reviewers' non-blocking remarks that fall inside point 39.

1. **Style of `goldstein_price()` in `examples/16_Goldstein_Price.cpp`.** The function kept the GAOL 4 style: integer pairs `interval(1,1)`, `interval(19,19)` and so on, and continuation lines indented with tabs. It now builds its constants with the numeric constructors (`interval(1.0)`, `interval(19.0)`, `interval(14.0)`...), puts spaces around the operators, and indents with spaces in the layout of `goldstein_price()` in `03_dependency_problem.cpp`. The expression is the same one in the same order of operations: `pow(., 2)`, left-to-right sums, `interval(6.0) * x * y`. So the natural extension is unchanged, and the check `z.set_eq(interval(-87881320.0, 147125080.0))` still passes on the three builds.
2. **Where x = -1.7373725377583070 comes from.** Example 16 credited mpmath, and example 03 credited sympy plus a numpy grid. I derived the point again in three ways:
   - **mpmath** (`$SCR/tmp/39/gp_mpmath.py`). On each edge of [-2, 2]^2, f is a polynomial of degree 8 with integer coefficients. It was expanded exactly, and the roots of its derivative (degree 7) were found with `mpmath.polyroots` at 50 digits and polished with `findroot`. The interior critical points were found with `findroot` on the gradient, started from a 41 x 41 grid. It found the 9 known stationary points, the largest value being 1155. Result: maximum 1015690.27179805890829884231208 at (-1.737372537758307011527846, 2), minimum 3 at (0, -1).
   - **sympy** (`real_roots` of the exact derivative on the four edges). It gives the same maximum, at x = -1.73737253775830701152784568384.
   - **numpy grid of 4001 x 4001 points.** Maximum 1015690.1611 at (-1.7370, 2), minimum 3 at (0, -1).

   sympy and numpy were installed with pip into `$SCR/tmp/39/py`, not into the system. All three agree with the digits in both examples. Both comments now name the same tools. The header of 03 says "found apart by solving df(x, 2)/dx = 0 exactly (sympy) and at 50 digits (mpmath), and confirmed by a grid of 4001 x 4001 points (numpy) and by an interval branch and bound". The paragraph was rewrapped from there to its end. Example 16 says "(sympy and mpmath; see 03_dependency_problem.cpp)": only two comment lines change.
3. **The unit of the time.** `elapsed_time()` returns milliseconds. `gaol_profile.cpp` computes them with `getrusage` as `ru_utime.tv_sec*1000 + tv_usec/1000`, or with `clock()*1000/CLOCKS_PER_SEC`, and the manual says "time in milliseconds". The output line is now `Elapsed time: 155 ms`. The manual's timing examples write `" ms."`, and `examples/examples.md` section 16 already says "in milliseconds".

The first output line (`<-0, 0>` in SSE2 and Clang, `<0, 0>` with the FPU build) is kept as it is: the maintainer decided that the zeros of interval operations are not normalized. `examples/examples.md` (section 3, "In short") was not touched, and neither was the manual, which prints no time.

## Open questions and decisions for the maintainer

1. `03_dependency_problem.cpp` line 237 and `06_global_optimization.cpp` line 337 write the upper bound of the true range as a double: `interval(1015690.2717980589082988423...)` and `interval(3.0, 1015690.2717980589082989)`.
