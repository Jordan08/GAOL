## What was wrong and what the change does

Point 31 asked for the fixes of the vendored CORE-MATH sources to be proposed
upstream, and for the same check on glibc. The maintainer decided to write them
in `3rd/README.md`. The change adds a section "Changes to propose upstream" to
`3rd/README.md`. It has a status table, a CORE-MATH part with five patches
against upstream master `b1a4badf` (29 September 2026) and one "already fixed"
note, and a glibc part. Each patch gives the defect, its place (file and line at
`b1a4badf`), the diff, how it was checked, and its status.

Findings, against current upstream master (CORE-MATH cloned from
gitlab.inria.fr, 10 commits after the vendored `6b84457`):

1. **Masks `~0ul>>12`** (GAOL item 2): still present in master, and not only
   in sinh/cosh/tanh. They are also in `sinpi.c` (99, 109), `log1p.c` (483) and
   `lgamma.c` (753); GAOL compiles sinpi and log1p and left them unchanged. The
   patch also turns `1l << (a->ex + 1073)` of `cos.c`, `tan.c` and `sincos.c`
   into `1ll`. That shift goes up to 51 places and is UB with a 32-bit long, but
   no argument reaches it. Analysis and measurement: a mask of 20 bits makes
   the rare-path tests weaker, not wrong. They trigger a database look-up or a
   one-ulp nudge of the low part, which is right anyway, so **no result
   changes**. With MinGW-w64 x64 (LLP64) under wine, sinh, cosh, tanh, sinpi,
   log1p and lgamma from master and from the patch equal x86-64 Linux, bit for
   bit, over their `.wc` arguments plus 1M random arguments each, in 4
   directions (the programs were compiled with -mfma; see open question 4).
   The inputs were 1.29M (sinh), 1.04M (cosh), 1.03M (tanh), 1.07M (sinpi),
   1.35M (log1p) and 2.62M (lgamma), all with 0 differences. The extra look-ups are 5, 11 and 1 per 4M random calls of sinh,
   cosh and tanh. So this is a correctness-of-intent and portability fix, not a
   wrong-result fix. The README had said "Upstream writes ~0ull in its other
   sources", which was false, and is corrected.
2. **asinpi_acc shift** (GAOL item 4): still present (line 273). UBSan (GCC 13)
   reproduces it on master: `asinpi.c:273:26: shift exponent 65 is too large`,
   for cr_asinpi(0x1.ffffffffff0c7p-1) in every direction. The patch is
   `D = {.a = (u128)dc << ss}`, the same value as GAOL's port. ss ranges from 25
   to 76 in asinpi_acc. Through cr_asinpi, master and the patch give identical
   results: over 1M arguments next to ±1 in 4 directions, and over the 19,041
   accurate-phase calls found among 4M more plus the neighbourhoods of
   sqrt(1-4^-j). 2,172 of those calls had ss > 63, and all 19,041 are correctly
   rounded (mpmath at 300 bits). **Caveat found**: asinpi_acc called
   *directly* at x = ±0x1.fffffffffffffp-1, in the downward and toward-zero
   directions, gets dc = -2^51 and ss = 76. Cm - D then wraps around 2^128, so
   the 128-bit shift (the patch, and GAOL's port) gives a wrong result where
   master's UB happens to give the right one. cr_asinpi rounds this x in its
   fast phase, so it never reaches asinpi_acc there. This is written in the
   README "for the authors".
3. **rsqrt `__builtin_expect(ix.u, 1)`** (GAOL item 5): still present (line
   172). It is reproduced with MinGW-w64 x64 under wine: master returns +inf in
   all 4 directions for all 149,797 subnormals tested whose low 32 bits are 0;
   the patch (`ix.u != 0`) returns Linux's results. Upstream's `exp2.c` already
   carries a comment warning about this exact pitfall. The README's item 5 said
   "2^-1040 to 2^-1024" and "2^520 to 2^512"; this is corrected to 2^-1042 and
   2^521, since 2^-1042 = 2^32 × 2^-1074 is affected too (10 powers of 4, as
   reproduced).
4. **sin.c `__builtin_roundeven`** (the port header's workaround): still present
   (line 829). The patch copies exp.c's `roundeven_finite()` block and calls it.
5. **pow's rounding direction** (from point 6, open question 4): `pow.h` 236,
   261 and 408 call `fegetround()`. The patch adds the x86-64 part of rsqrt.c's
   `get_rounding_mode()`, which reads MXCSR. It is reproduced with the x87 unit
   to nearest and MXCSR upward: master rounds the 3 subnormal cases of point 6
   down, and the patch rounds them up (GCC and Clang). cos.c, tan.c and
   sincos.c carry the same code, but it is unreachable there (never subnormal),
   so they are left alone.
6. **cospi signed shifts** (GAOL item 3): **already fixed upstream** by
   `b1a4badf` ("[cospi] make the sanitizer happy", 29 September 2026), which
   makes `m` a `uint64_t`. Its results equal GAOL's (16M results), and it is
   UBSan-clean where 6b84457 is not. Nothing to send.

glibc (shallow clone of master `30f988fbe795` plus the tags 2.40 to 2.44):
CORE-MATH files appear from 2.41 (float functions only), 2.43 (acosh, asinh,
atanh, erf, erfc, lgamma, tgamma of doubles), 2.44 (+ cosh, sinh, tanh) and
master (+ cbrt). **None of the defects is present in any release or in
master**:

- glibc's cosh, sinh and tanh use `MANTISSA_MASK`
  (`UINT64_C(0x000fffffffffffff)`), and lgamma uses `~UINT64_C(0) >> 12`. The
  `__glibc_(un)likely` of these files take comparisons only.
- asinpi, rsqrt and cospi of doubles are glibc's generic templates; sin and pow
  are other code.
- The rounding direction: inside glibc, `get_rounding_mode()` (generic header,
  `_FPU_GETCW` = `fnstcw` on x86) and `fegetround()` both read the x87 unit,
  by design ("The SSE unit should be the same"), and `fesetround()` sets both.
  Only cbrt, among its CORE-MATH double functions, reads it. Nothing to
  propose.

So no glibc patch is written. The README gives the procedure for a future need
(git send-email to libc-alpha, a `math:` subject, a Signed-off-by under the
DCO, a test in `math/auto-libm-test-in`).

## What was wrong and what the change does

The review of point 6 found that CORE-MATH's `cbrt.c` gets the rounding
direction wrong with mingw-w64 on x86-64. Checked on upstream master
`b1a4badf` (the three files are identical in the vendored `6b84457`):

- `get_rounding_mode()` of `cbrt.c`, `rsqrt.c` and `asinpi.c` (lines 84-101,
  84-101, 93-110) turns the rounding field of MXCSR into the libc's `FE_*`
  value with a shift chosen per libc: `>>3` (glibc values), or, under
  `__WIN32__`/`__WIN64__`, `>>5` (UCRT, `FE_UPWARD` 0x200) or a table (old
  UCRT, 0x100), and otherwise `#warning` + `return fegetround();`.
- **mingw-w64** defines `__WIN32__` and has `FE_UPWARD` 0x800 (its `fenv.h`),
  so it takes the last branch. `rsqrt.c`/`asinpi.c` compare the value with
  `FE_*` and are right while the x87 unit and MXCSR agree (mingw-w64 11's
  `fegetround()` is `fnstcw` only: disassembled from `libmingwex.a`). `cbrt.c`
  needs 0..3, which its final switch computes, and the `return` skips it:
  `rm` = 0x400/0x800/0xc00, `off[rm]` is read 8-24 KiB past a 4-element array,
  `rm+sign == 2` never holds. Introduced by upstream `49d77f99` (5 Feb 2026,
  "Fix handling of FE_UPWARD on Windows pre-14393 update"), which copied the
  branch of `rsqrt.c` into `cbrt.c`.
- **clang-cl** defines `__x86_64__`, `_WIN32`, `_WIN64`, `_MSC_VER`, `_M_X64`
  but not `__WIN32__` (checked with `clang-cl-18 /E -Xclang -dM`), so all
  three shift by 3 and compare 0x400/0x800/0xc00 with the UCRT's
  0x100/0x200/0x300: downward and upward are taken for toward zero.

**Patch 6** (new, `[cbrt,rsqrt,asinpi] read the rounding field of MXCSR
whatever the values of FE_*`): on x86-64 the three functions read the field
as the hardware defines it (0 nearest, 1 down, 2 up, 3 toward zero): `cbrt.c`
returns it directly (it is its 0..3), `rsqrt.c` and `asinpi.c` look up
`{FE_TONEAREST, FE_DOWNWARD, FE_UPWARD, FE_TOWARDZERO}[field]`, the table the
upstream `FE_UPWARD == 0x0100` branch already uses. No `#if` on the platform,
no `#warning`, no `fegetround()` on x86-64. The smallest fix
(`mode = fegetround();` in `cbrt.c`, what point 6 does in GAOL's copy) is
mentioned in the README as fixing mingw-w64 only.

**Patch 5 corrected (an error in the first pass).** Patch 5 (`pow.h`) had
copied `rsqrt.c`'s `get_rounding_mode()`. That copy (a) still calls
mingw-w64's `fegetround()`, which reads the x87 unit, so it did not fix the
x87/MXCSR disagreement with mingw-w64 (checked: same 97k wrong results per
direction as master under wine), and (b) would *break* `pow` with clang-cl,
where master is right (emulated: 193,196 wrong upward, 1,168 downward). It now
has the table form of patch 6; subject changed to `[pow] read the rounding
mode from MXCSR on x86-64 for the subnormal results`. The README text of
patch 5 now says mingw-w64's `fegetround()` reads the x87 unit too.

**glibc**: its `cbrt` (master only; 2.44's `s_cbrt.c` is the old FSF one, and
i386 uses `s_cbrt.S`) calls `cbrt_rounding_index()`, a switch over
`get_rounding_mode()` mapping glibc's four `FE_*` to 0..3 with
`__builtin_unreachable()` as default; glibc's `get_rounding_mode()`
(`sysdeps/generic/get-rounding-mode.h`) reads the x87 control word with
`_FPU_GETCW` (`fnstcw`) and maps `_FPU_RC_*` to `FE_*`, aborting on anything
else. No raw value reaches `off[]`, no per-libc branch: not affected. Written
in the glibc part and in the status table.

README changes: intro ("three more found while preparing them or since"),
status table row 6, toolchains paragraph (Clang 18 for mingw for patches 5
and 6; the paragraph rewrapped), patch 5 (defect text, diff block, subject,
