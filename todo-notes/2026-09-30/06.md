## What was wrong and what the change does

On x86-64 with glibc, CORE-MATH's `pow` rounds a subnormal result itself
(`pow.h` lines 236, 261 and 408), in the direction `fegetround()` gives, and
glibc's `fegetround()` reads the x87 control word only. GAOL's probe
(1 + 2^-60 > 1) sees MXCSR only, so with the x87 unit to nearest and MXCSR
upward (the state `exactinit()` of Shewchuk's predicates and of Triangle
leaves), GAOL left the x87 unit alone and `pow` rounded its subnormal results
to nearest: the upper bound was below the exact value for about half of the
arguments.

The fix (first pass, kept as is): `gaol/core_math_port.h`, which the three
builds force-include into the CORE-MATH sources only, defines on x86-64
(`__x86_64__` or `_M_X64`), after `<fenv.h>` and `<xmmintrin.h>`, a
`static inline gaol_fegetround()` that reads the RC field of MXCSR and maps it
through a switch to the `FE_*` constants of the C library, then
`#undef fegetround` / `#define fegetround gaol_fegetround`.

This pass:

1. Merged `origin/configure-clean` (conflicts in `doc/tests.md` and
   `tests/rounding_direction.cpp`, both sides kept: the subnormal `pow` block
   first, then the `is_empty()` / FE_INVALID checks of point 24; the header
   comment of the test keeps both paragraphs).
2. Reviewed the change (details below) and fixed two comments:
   - `gaol/gaol_fpu.h`, `round_upward_if_needed()`: the sentence "The C
     library computes in the direction GAOL sets before calling it
     (round_nearest()), whatever the x87 unit had" was stale. It dates from
     when GAOL evaluated its elementary functions with the C library to
     nearest; they are now CORE-MATH's, called in the upward direction without
     `round_nearest()`, and CORE-MATH reading `fegetround()` from the x87 unit
     was exactly the hole of point 6. It now says why the x87 unit may keep
     another direction on x86-64: it computes none of GAOL's doubles, GAOL sets
     both units before the C library reads or writes a number
     (`round_nearest()`), and CORE-MATH reads MXCSR there
     (`gaol/core_math_port.h`).
   - `gaol/core_math_port.h`: the comment said CORE-MATH's own
     `get_rounding_mode()` of `cbrt.c`, `rsqrt.c` and `asinpi.c` reads MXCSR;
     with mingw-w64 (whose `FE_UPWARD` is 0x0800, neither of the two Windows
     values it knows) it calls `fegetround()` instead. Said so.

### Review

- MXCSR read and mapping: RC is bits 13-14 of MXCSR; `_MM_ROUND_MASK`
  (0x6000), `_MM_ROUND_NEAREST` (0), `_MM_ROUND_DOWN` (0x2000), `_MM_ROUND_UP`
  (0x4000) are the same in the `<xmmintrin.h>` of GCC, Clang and Visual C++;
  the `default` is toward zero (0x6000). The switch returns the libc's own
  `FE_*` constants, so it is right for glibc and musl and Apple (0x400/0x800),
  mingw-w64 (0x400/0x800/0xc00, checked here with the mingw-w64 headers) and
  the UCRT (0x100/0x200/0x300, swapped once). The guard `__x86_64__ ||
  _M_X64` covers Visual C++ x64, clang-cl (defines both, checked with
  `clang-18 --target=x86_64-pc-windows-msvc -dM -E`), MinGW x64 and MSYS2
  CLANG64 (`__x86_64__`), Apple x86_64 and musl. It is the guard GAOL itself
  uses to read MXCSR in `gaol/gaol_fpu.h`.
- Placement: the macro comes after `<fenv.h>` is fully included, and nothing
  after it declares `fegetround` again. Checked by preprocessing the thirty-six
  sources GAOL builds with GCC 13, clang-18 and mingw-w64 (GCC 13, mingw-w64
  11 headers): the only remaining `fegetround` token is the libc declaration,
  no declaration is renamed, and `gaol_fegetround` appears only in its
  definition and the calls. `fegetround` is declared `__attribute_pure__` by
  glibc; `_mm_getcsr()` is not CSE'd across a change of MXCSR (stmxcsr is
  volatile for GCC and Clang), so the read is not hoisted either.
- Sources that read the rounding direction (grep of the thirty-six `<f>.c` and
  the headers they include for `fegetround`, `FE_*`, `get_rounding_mode`,
  `_mm_getcsr`, `FLT_ROUNDS`, `fegetenv`, `fesetround`):
  - `pow.h` (3 calls), `cos.c` and `tan.c` (`subnormalize_dint()`): call
    `fegetround()`, now MXCSR on every x86-64 libc.
  - `cbrt.c`, `rsqrt.c`, `asinpi.c`: `get_rounding_mode()` reads MXCSR itself
    on x86-64, except with mingw-w64 where it falls into `#warning ...
    return fegetround();`, now MXCSR too. With Visual C++ x64 (no
    `__x86_64__`) `cbrt.c` calls `fegetround()` in its generic branch, now
    MXCSR.
  - `exp.c`, `exp2.c`, `exp10.c`, `pow.c`, `hypot.c`: `_mm_getcsr()` for the
    exception flags only.
  - no `FLT_ROUNDS`, `fegetenv` or `fesetround` in the compiled sources.
  So no source GAOL compiles reads the x87 unit on x86-64 any more: `nm` of
  `pow`, `cos`, `tan`, `cbrt`, `rsqrt`, `asinpi` compiled for mingw-w64 x64,
  and of `pow`, `cos`, `tan` for Linux with `-std=c99 -pedantic`, shows no
  `fegetround` reference; the Linux objects use `stmxcsr`.
- The test: the three references were recomputed apart with mpmath at 800
  bits (1458662373659.2900063, 567863.25606684497, 1.3000000000970768 times
  2^-1074; floor and ceiling as in the test). `subnormal()` is exact in every
  direction (a multiple of 2^-1074 below 2^-1022). The six states include
  "to nearest, and upward for SSE" where GAOL_TESTS_SSE is set; elsewhere the
  four directions only, where the checks still hold.
- i386: left out, as the first pass argued (`round_upward_if_needed()` sets
  both units when either is not upward, and GAOL calls CORE-MATH only in the
  upward direction, so the two units agree whenever CORE-MATH runs). The
  comments of `gaol_fpu.h` about 32-bit x86 remain right.

## What was wrong and what the change does

`get_rounding_mode()` of `3rd/math-core/src/binary64/cbrt/cbrt.c` must return
0 to 3 (nearest, downward, upward, toward zero): `cr_cbrt()` indexes
`off[4]` with it and rounds the seven arguments of `wlist` away from zero when
`rm + sign == 2`. Under `__x86_64__` with `__WIN32__` defined it computes the
`FE_*` value from MXCSR for the two Windows layouts (`FE_UPWARD` 0x200 or
0x100) and maps it through the final switch; with any other value, which is
mingw-w64's (`FE_UPWARD` 0x800, glibc's layout), it did `#warning ...
return fegetround();`, returning the raw `FE_*` value past the switch. In the
directed roundings `off[0x400/0x800/0xc00]` was read 8 to 24 KiB beyond the
array and the seven hard cases were rounded toward zero, so the upper bound of
`nth_root(x, 3)` (and `rootn`) was one double below the cube root, on every
MinGW-w64 and MSYS2 (UCRT64, CLANG64) on x64.

The change is one line of the vendored source, marked:
`return fegetround();` -> `mode = fegetround(); /* GAOL: mapped to 0..3 by
the switch below, not returned */`. The `fegetround()` there is GAOL's MXCSR
read (`gaol/core_math_port.h`, point 6), so the value is the direction of the
SSE unit in mingw-w64's `FE_*` values, which the switch maps. Preprocessed for
mingw-w64 x64 the function is now `*flagp = _mm_getcsr(); mode =
gaol_fegetround(); switch (mode) {0x0000: 0; 0x0400: 1; 0x0800: 2} return 3`.
Upstream master (checked on gitlab.inria.fr, last cbrt.c commit 8ae55cd,
2026-09-21) still has the bug: a fix to propose to CORE-MATH (point 31).

### Audit of every rounding-direction reader of the 36 sources GAOL builds

Only `cbrt.c`, `rsqrt.c` and `asinpi.c` have a `get_rounding_mode()`; `pow.h`,
`cos.c` and `tan.c` call `fegetround()` and compare with `FE_*` (MXCSR on
x86-64 since point 6); `exp.c`, `exp2.c`, `exp10.c`, `pow.c`, `hypot.c` use
`_mm_getcsr()` for flags only. For the three:

| Platform (macros) | branch taken | cbrt (needs 0..3) | rsqrt, asinpi (need `FE_*`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linux glibc, Alpine musl, macOS x86_64 (`__x86_64__`) | `RC >> 3` = 0/0x400/0x800/0xc00, the libc's values | switch: right | right |
| MinGW-w64 GCC, MSYS2 UCRT64/CLANG64 x64 (`__x86_64__`, `__WIN32__`, mingw-w64 `FE_UPWARD` 0x800) | `#else` fallback `fegetround()` | **wrong, fixed** | right (they return `FE_*`) |
| Visual C++ x64 (`_M_X64`, no `__x86_64__`) | generic: `fegetround()` (GAOL's MXCSR read) | switch: right | right |
| Visual C++ / clang-cl arm64, macOS arm64, MinGW arm64 | FPCR, lut of `FE_*` | switch: right | right |
| i386 (Debian, MinGW x86, Visual C++ x86), armhf, s390x, ppc64le, riscv64 | generic `fegetround()` of the libc | switch: right | right |
| **clang-cl x64** (`__x86_64__`, `_WIN32`, no `__WIN32__`, UCRT `FE_UPWARD` 0x200) | `RC >> 3` compared with the UCRT's 0x100/0x200/0x300 | **wrong** | **wrong** |

clang-cl is not fixed: see open question A. The `off[rm]` out-of-bounds read
is gone wherever the fix applies.

## Behaviour change

`cbrt()` of `textToInterval` enclose the cube root at the seven `wlist`

too high below for negative bounds), and `cr_cbrt` no longer reads beyond

## Open questions and decisions for the maintainer

A. **clang-cl x64 (not fixed, not in the CI).** clang-cl defines
   `__x86_64__` and `_WIN32` but not `__WIN32__` (checked with `clang-18
   --target=x86_64-pc-windows-msvc -dM -E`), so the three `get_rounding_mode()`
   compute `RC >> 3` (0x400/0x800/0xc00) and compare it with the UCRT's
   `FE_*` (0x100/0x200/0x300). The cbrt fix does not cover it. Emulated on
   Linux (`tmp/06/clangcl/`: the three sources compiled with clang-18 and a
   header putting the UCRT values on `FE_*`, against the same sources compiled
   normally, 4 directions, the `wlist` arguments, 11 asinpi arguments next to
   1, powers of 2 and neighbours and 200,000 random arguments): 21 cbrt results
   wrong upward and 21 downward, 1,026 rsqrt results wrong upward, and about
   100,000 asinpi results wrong in each of upward and downward (about half).
   GAOL computes upward, so its bounds of `rsqrt`, `asinpi` and `nth_root(x,
   3)` would not enclose. The smallest fix, one token in each of the three
   files, `#if defined(__WIN32__) || defined(__WIN64__) || defined(_WIN32) /*
   GAOL */`, sends clang-cl to the Windows branch (`FE_UPWARD == 0x0200`,
   `>> 5`): the emulation then gives 0 differences. It changes nothing for
   the compilers of the CI (Visual C++ defines no `__x86_64__`, mingw-w64
   already defines `__WIN32__`, Cygwin defines no `_WIN32`). Per the task it
   is reported, not committed, since the cbrt change does not cover it.
   Recommended if clang-cl is to be accepted; otherwise GAOL could refuse
   clang-cl on x64 (`gaol/gaol_config.h`, `defined(_MSC_VER) &&
   defined(__clang__) && defined(__x86_64__)`) until then.
B. The `#warning` of the three files still fires with mingw-w64 (upstream
   text, kept to stay close to upstream).
C. As in report 06b: mingw-w64's own `fma` makes one direct `cbrt` check fail
   (downward, negative argument); it does not affect GAOL's bounds here, but
   it affects many other functions (06b, open question 1).

## For doc/differences.md

- **`nth_root(x, 3)` encloses the cube root with MinGW-w64 and MSYS2 on
  x64.** CORE-MATH's `cbrt` rounds seven hard arguments (and their products
  by powers of 8) apart, from a direction coded 0 to 3; with mingw-w64 on
  x86-64 its `get_rounding_mode()` returned the value of `fegetround()`
  instead (0x800 for upward), so that these were rounded toward zero and the
  upper bound was one double below the cube root, e.g.
  `nth_root([0x1.3a9ccd7f022dbp+0], 3) = [0x1.1236160ba9b92p+0,
  0x1.1236160ba9b93p+0]`. The value now goes through the switch of the other
  branches (change 6 of `3rd/README.md`); `tests/core_math.cpp` checks the 70
  arguments against mpmath.
