## What was wrong and what the change does

Point 24 (PR #47) made `is_empty()` and `interval::emptyset()` quiet. Many
operations still raised the invalid-operation exception on the NaN bounds of
an empty operand, and died on SIGFPE under `feenableexcept(FE_INVALID)`:
either they compared a bound with `<`, `<=`, `>=`, `>` (signaling comparisons,
`comisd` on x86), or they built their result from the NaN bounds with the
constructor `interval(l, r)`, which compares them.

**Survey.** I redid it over the whole public interface of `gaol::interval`
and `gaol_ieee1788` (scratchpad `tmp/24b/survey.cpp`): 150 operations, 372
(operation, position of the empty operand) pairs in the SSE build and 371 in
the FPU build (every member, relation, predicate, arithmetic operator with an
interval or a double, every function, the reverse functions, the three
`gaol_ieee1788` wrappers that add code, the outputs in the five formats, the
readers of `[empty]` and of expressions giving an empty set), each binary one
against 10 partners ([1,2], [-2,-1], [-1,2], [0], [0,+oo], [-oo,0],
[-oo,+oo], [1,+oo], [0.5], empty), each ternary one against 25; the flag
tested in process (`fetestexcept(FE_INVALID)`) and the trap in a child with
`feenableexcept(FE_INVALID)`. On the base: **64 pairs raise and trap with the
SSE2 intervals, 70 with the FPU ones**, the same at -O3 (Release library,
program -O3) and at -O0 (Debug library, program -O0), and with the program
compiled by clang-18 -O3. The list of the point-24 report was incomplete; the
full one:

- first operand empty: `is_canonical`, `straddles_zero` (new), `strictly_straddles_zero`,
  `set_contains(I)`, `set_contains(double)`, `set_strictly_contains(double)` (new), `set_disjoint`,
  `set_geq`, `split`, `split_left`/`split_right` (new), `sqrt` and through it `nth_root(x, 2)`,
  `nth_root(x, -2)`, `rootn(x, -2)`, `gaol_ieee1788::sqrt`, `exp`, `min`, `max`, `floor`, `ceil`,
  `integer` (new), `chi`, `modulo_k_pi` (new), `sqrt_rel` and through it `sqrRev`, `pownRev(c, 2)`,
  `nth_root_rel(c, 2, x)`, `isMember(m, x)` (new), `operator<<` in the bounds format,
  `operator std::string`, `intervalToText`, `textToInterval("[empty]", "[1, 2]")` (new);
  FPU only: unary minus, `neg`, and `cancel_plus`/`cancelPlus` (which negate);
- second operand empty: `x & y`, `x &= y`, `intersection`, and every function intersecting
  its result with it: `asinh_rel`, `atanh_rel`, `invabs_rel`, `nth_root_rel(c, 1, x)`, `sinhRev`,
  `tanhRev`, `absRev` (new); `set_disjoint`, `set_leq`/`subset`, `min`, `max`, `sqrt_rel`.

**The change**, each at no or least cost on nonempty operands:

- Quiet comparisons of `<cmath>` where a comparison was the culprit, same
  instruction count (`ucomisd` for `comisd`): `straddles_zero`,
  `strictly_straddles_zero`, `set_contains(I)`, `set_contains(double)`,
  `set_strictly_contains(double)`, `set_disjoint`, `set_leq` (so `set_geq`,
  `subset`, `disjoint`, `isMember`), `operator&=` in `gaol_interval_sse.h`
  and `gaol_interval_fpu.h` (so `&`, `intersection` and the reverse functions),
  `is_canonical` (non-MSVC branch; MSVC already tested `is_empty()` first),
  `display_bounds` (`operator<<`, `std::string`, `intervalToText`), `chi`
  (whose result for the empty set stays NaN).
- FPU unary minus: the stored bounds are exchanged, as the SSE2 intervals do,
  instead of going through the constructor: bit-identical for every interval,
  quiet, and 4 instructions instead of 17 (clang-18) / 9 instead of 20 (GCC).
- The empty set told before the constructor gets NaN bounds, with the
  cheapest test measured for each: `min`, `max`, `floor`, `ceil`, `integer`
  test the computed bounds with one `std::isunordered(l, r)` (NaN for an empty
  operand); `split`, `split_left`, `split_right` test `std::isnan(midpoint())`;
  `sqrt` and `sqrt_rel` take the part in [0, +oo] as `I & interval::positive()`
  (the quiet intersection, as `nth_root()` does) instead of
  `interval(maximum(0, l), r)`, which also saves the call to `maximum()`;
  `exp`, `modulo_k_pi` (NaN, NaN, 0 as before) and `textToInterval(sl, sr)`
  test `is_empty()` first.

Every result is unchanged (the result checks of the new test pass on the base
code, ctest passes); only the exception is gone. After the change the survey
finds **0 pairs raising or trapping in all five configurations** (GCC SSE and
FPU at -O3, SSE and FPU Debug at -O0, clang-18 SSE -O3).

- The quiet comparisons cost nothing (`&=`, `set_contains`, `set_disjoint`,
  `set_leq`, `straddles_zero`, `chi`): same instruction count with clang-18,
  `ucomisd` for `comisd`. With GCC 13 the effect of `x &= y` depends on the
  loop, and is not a speed-up in general: 6.43 -> 2.45 ns in this loop, but
  +13 to +29% in the reviewer's throughput loop (see "Review round"); GCC used
  `cmplesd`/`blendvpd` for the signaling comparison, `ucomisd`/`cmov` and a
  branch now. `set_disjoint` 1.43 -> 1.19, `straddles_zero` 0.96 -> 0.83.
- `min`, `max`, `sqrt`: within the layout noise (one layout faster, the other
  slower). The variants were compared in one binary and by swapping only the
  function in the after-library: testing `I` and `J` with `is_empty()` first
  made `max` 5 to 10% slower, one `isunordered` on the computed bounds costs 2
  instructions and nothing measurable; for `sqrt`, `is_empty()` first cost
  +0.9 ns, a quiet test of the upper bound first +0.3 ns, `I & positive()`
  9.40-9.63 vs 9.23-9.42 ns (aligned) and 9.59-9.64 vs 10.01-10.08 ns
  (default).
- `exp` (+0.2 ns, <1%), `split` (+0.1 ns, 0.5%) and `split_left` (+0.3 ns,
  2%): the emptiness test, at the level of the noise for `exp` and `split`;
  for `split_left` the cost shows in both layouts, although `isnan(midpoint())`
  was faster than the original (16.0 vs 16.4 ns) and than `is_empty()` first
  (16.6 ns) when the variants were compared as functions of one binary.
- **`floor` and `ceil` (and `integer`): +0.17 ns, 11%, in a loop doing
  nothing else** (GCC: 1.43 -> 1.60). The one place the fix is not free: the
  constructor compares its bounds, and without changing it (out of scope) one
  comparison has to be added; `isunordered` on the computed bounds was the
  cheapest of four variants (`is_empty()` first: +0.3 to +0.4 ns). See the
  open questions for the two ways to make it free.

## Behaviour change

A program that enabled the invalid-operation exception is no longer killed by
an empty operand anywhere in the interface (about 50 operations, among them
`x & y` with an empty `y`, the common `x &= f(x)` of contractors), and a
program reading the flags no longer sees `FE_INVALID` after them. The values
of all results are unchanged, bit for bit for well-formed intervals; with the
FPU intervals, unary minus exchanges the stored bounds instead of rebuilding
them (same bits). The exceptions raised on nonempty operands (0*oo in the SSE2
product, `pow` with a subnormal exponent, divide-by-zero, overflow, inexact)
and by a NaN the program supplies are unchanged.

## Risks

- Visual C++ builds the FPU intervals, and its `std::islessequal()` and
  siblings (UCRT `corecrt_math.h`) are, as far as I know, templates over
  `_fpcomp()`, which calls `_dpcomp()` in the C runtime: a call where GCC and
  Clang emit one `ucomisd`. I could not check nor measure it here; point 24's
  `is_empty()` already made that choice. The CI's Windows jobs run the test
  (in-process flag part); a slowdown would not show there.
- The trap checks run on glibc only; the in-process flag checks everywhere.
  They rely on `std::isless()`... being quiet, as C99/C++11 require; checked
  here with GCC 13 and clang-18 (x86-64, SSE2 and FPU intervals); the
  MinGW-w64 installed here is one GAOL refuses (`gaol_config.h`, point 6), and
  its `<cmath>` maps them to the same GCC builtins. On AArch64 Clang, `<` was
  already quiet, so the checks are vacuous there.
- `floor`/`ceil`/`min`/`max` test the computed bounds for NaN, so a malformed
  interval (inverted non-NaN bounds, which only `interval(__m128d)` could
  build) goes to the constructor as before; `sqrt` of such an interval would
  raise the flag (its result stays empty). GAOL's own operations never build
  one (point 24/earlier: disjoint intersections give NaN bounds).
- The survey covers the public operations with the empty set built by GAOL;
  the expression classes (`gaol_expression.h`) evaluate through the same
  operations and were only reached through the parser.

## Open questions and decisions for the maintainer

1. `floor`, `ceil`, `integer` cost +0.17 ns (1.52 -> 1.69 ns, clang-18; 1.43
   -> 1.60 with GCC) in a loop of `floor` only. To make them free: (a) make
   the first comparison of the constructor `interval(l, r)` quiet
   (`std::islessequal(l, r)`, same cost): then `floor`, `ceil`, `integer`,
   `min`, `max`, `split` need no test at all, but `interval(NaN, x)` becomes
   quiet too, which this task was told to leave as it is; or (b) let them
   build `[floor(l), floor(r)]` from the stored bounds without the
   constructor's three comparisons (friends of `interval`, as `sqrt`, `cos`,
   `sin` are; `_mm_set_pd` with the SSE2 intervals, `lb_`/`rb_` with the FPU
   ones): faster than before, NaN bounds giving the empty set by themselves,
   but SSE/FPU code in `gaol_interval.h`. I kept the minimal form.
2. Left as they are (out of scope, as asked): `interval(NaN)`,
   `interval(NaN, x)`, `interval(x, NaN)` and the operations given a NaN
   double by the program (`x += NaN`, `set_contains(NaN)`...), which raise
   `FE_INVALID`; `0 * oo` in the SSE2 `operator*=` and `pow` with a subnormal
   or extreme exponent (nonempty operands, documented); `GAOL_PRESERVE_ROUNDING`
   masking the exceptions again in the SSE2 operations (point 24, question 2).
3. The developer-only float intervals (`gaol_intervalf.h`,
   `gaol_interval2f.h`) are not changed: their `&=` and relations still use
   signaling comparisons.
4. Visual C++: see Risks; if its quiet comparisons turn out to be calls, a
   GAOL helper using `_mm_ucomile_sd` and its siblings on x86/x64 would give
   the single instruction back (for `is_empty()` too).

### Open questions added by this round

1. `floor`, `ceil`, `integer`: +0.1 to +0.25 ns (8-17%, reproduced by the
   reviewer with clang-18 and GCC 13); making them free needs (a) a quiet first
   comparison in the constructor or (b) building the result without it
   (SSE2/FPU code in `gaol_interval.h`). The maintainer's choice.
2. `x &= y` compiled by GCC 13: +13 to +29% in a throughput loop (branch and
   `cmov` instead of `vcmplesd`/`vblendvpd`), unchanged with clang-18. A
   branchless quiet form would need AVX (`_CMP_LE_OQ`) or integer tricks;
   not attempted.
3. Visual C++: the speed of `std::islessequal()` and siblings (four in the
   FPU `&=` now) is not known; `gaol_performance` measures neither `&=` nor
   the relations.
4. New point, existing bug (not from this branch, found by the reviewer):
   `cancel_minus`/`cancel_plus` in the GCC 13 Release library.
   `cancel_minus([0.5], [4.9e-324, 1e-300])` gives `[0x1.fffffffffffffp-2,
   0.5]` instead of Entire (IEEE 1788-2015 12.12.5: y wider than x), and
   `cancel_minus([DBL_MAX], [0.5])` gives Entire instead of
   `[pred(DBL_MAX), DBL_MAX]`; the Debug and clang builds are right. The
   reviewer's objdump shows GCC -O3 computing the `two_sum` error terms of
   `difference_at_least()` after `GAOL_RND_NEAREST_LEAVE()` (upward);
   `GAOL_RND_KEEP` on `s1`/`e1`/`s2`/`e2` would pin them. No test catches it
   (`tmp/review-24b/cm.cpp`).
