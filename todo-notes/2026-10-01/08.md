## Review of the WIP commit

The WIP commit had the right correction (`nl = std::fma(-h,h,p) + nl*(2.0*h - nl);`,
sound: `2h - nl` rounded upward is at least the exact value, `nl >= 0`, so the
product and the sum rounded upward are upper bounds of the exact deficit) and a
rewritten comment. What was wrong or missing:

- the comment said the square of nl is "about (n 2^-52)^2 h", which is not
  homogeneous (it is up to (k 2^-52)^2 x^2k for x^k);
- the comment and the docs kept the claim "one double beyond where the power
  is within n 2^-104 of a double", which is false: measured, the unrounded
  bounds are up to 1.3 n 2^-104 from x^n already for n = 4 to 7, and 53 n 2^-104
  for n near 2^32 (see below);
- nothing about the second half of the point (SSE2 and FPU bounds differing);
- no test, no documentation, no validation.

## What was wrong and what the change does

1. **Lower bound of large powers.** `ipow_exact_dn()` carries the power as
   h - nl and, squaring it, took nl' = (p - h*h) + 2h*nl, leaving out the
   +nl^2 of (h - nl)^2 = p - ((p - h*h) + nl*(2h - nl)). Sound, but the lower
   bound drifted: 8 doubles below the tightest for pow([1.0000001], 2^28 - 1),
   557 for 2^31 - 1, 1962 for 2^32 - 1 (the numbers of the review, reproduced).
   Over 20 000 random x and n (x^n from 2^-950 to 2^1015), the old lower bound
   was more than one double below the tightest for 13/678 n of 27 bits, 210/622
   of 28 bits, 516/687 of 29 bits, 664/696 of 30 bits, 650/659 of 31 bits and
   696/699 of 32 bits (worst 3372 doubles). The fix keeps nl^2 (the WIP line).
   With it, 4000 random (x, n) with n from 2^20 to 2^32 - 1, and their
   opposites, give the tightest bounds in both builds.

2. **The error bound of the exact products.** Derivation (directed rounding,
   u = 2^-52): the carried rest of x^k is below (k - 1) u x^k; a squaring
   doubles the relative error and adds at most 6 u delta + u^2 (three roundings
   of terms below 2 (k - 1) u x^2k and the rest of p), a product by x adds
   2 u delta + u^2. Normalising by k u^2, each squaring adds less than 3 and each
   product less than 2: the unrounded h + l and h - nl are within
   (3s + 2m) n 2^-104 of x^n relatively (s squarings, m products by x), at most
   5 log2(n) n 2^-104 (below 2^-64 for every n < 2^32, so still at most one
   double beyond). Measured over 20 000 random x and n from 3 to 2^32 - 1: at
   most 1.7 log2(n) n 2^-104 (e.g. 1.30 n 2^-104 for n = 4..7, 52.9 n 2^-104 for
   32-bit n). The comment, `doc/accuracy.md` and the manual now say
   "within 5 n log2(n) 2^-104 of a double, relatively" instead of n 2^-104.

3. **SSE2 and FPU bounds.** Where `pow(x, n)` takes the products rounded
   outward (n = 1, 2; for both bounds where one bound is 0 or infinite or its
   power is below 2^-968 or beyond the largest double), the SSE2 intervals
   multiplied from the highest bit of n (`MSB_position()`/`reverse_bits()`),
   the FPU intervals from the lowest bit: different products, different
   bounds. Measured: 742 of 1600 targeted random intervals, 3726 of 200 000
   random `pow(X, n)`/`pow(X, -n)` over any doubles and special values
   (n <= 40), differ in value. Both orders are as accurate (mean distance from
   the tightest 6.25 and 6.28 doubles, max 78 and 89, over 12 000 bounds with
   n up to 62).
   Choice: the SSE2 intervals now use the FPU order, which GAOL's own
   right-to-left SSE2 code already implemented under `#if 0` in
   `gaol_interval_sse.cpp`: the change swaps the two `#if` blocks (the old
   MSB-first code is kept under `#if 0`, as GAOL kept the other). It is the
   sound and simplest option and costs nothing: it is faster (Clang 18,
   pinned core, minimum of 8 runs): pow([0,1.5],3) 9.70 -> 7.24 ns,
   pow([0,1.5],7) 10.36 -> 8.56, pow([0,1.5],16) 13.42 -> 11.16,
   pow([-1.5,0],5) 15.12 -> 14.04, pow([2^-400,2],3) 10.39 -> 7.86,
   pow([1e200,1e201],3) 9.72 -> 7.18, pow([-1e200,2],7) 11.38 -> 10.42; the exact
   path is unchanged (16.18 / 16.24 ns). The alternative, the FPU intervals in
   the SSE2 order, was implemented and measured first, then dropped: it made
   the FPU fallback 0.6 to 3.9 ns slower (the squarings wait for the products).
   After the change: 0 value differences over 80 000 targeted and 200 000 broad
   random cases. The FPU code is unchanged (a comment says to change both or
   neither). For n = 1 and 2 the two orders compute the same products, so
   `sqr()` and pow(x, 2) are unchanged.
   The sign of a zero bound still differs (e.g. `sqr([-2, 3])` is [-0, 9] with
   SSE2 intervals, [0, 9] with FPU intervals; even powers of intervals
   containing 0 in the rounded products likewise): left as is, per the decision
   of 30 September not to normalise zeros, and written in `doc/accuracy.md`.

Cost of the fix itself (Clang 18, SSE2, min of 8): pow([1.1,1.5],3) 10.80 vs
10.80 ns, pow([1.1,1.5],7) 15.07 vs 15.23, pow([1.0000001], 2^20) 110.9 vs
112.3: within noise for small n, about 1 % for large n.

## Tests

`tests/arithmetic.cpp`, two new functions called from `main()`:

- `large_powers()`: 13 powers x^n with n from 2^24 + 1 to 2^32 - 1 (2^28 - 1,
  2^31 - 1, 2^32 - 1, 2^31, 2^32 - 2, 3000000019, 1000000007, 2^24 + 1 for
  x = 1.0000001; 2^31 - 1 and 2^32 - 1 for 0.9999999; 2^32 - 1 for 1 + 2^-24,
  for 2^(1023.5/n) next to the largest double and 2^(-967/n) next to 2^-968).
  The references are computed apart two ways that agree: mpmath at 2000 bits
  (snippet in the comment) and exact integer arithmetic (the n-th power of the
  integer significand bounded from below and above on 300 bits at each
  product). Each power is more than 71 times 5 n log2(n) 2^-104 from a double,
  so the tightest bounds are required, exactly. Checked: `gaol::pow([x], n)`
  (unsigned), `pow([x], int n)` where n fits, `pow([-x], n)` (odd: [-hi, -lo],
  even: [lo, hi]), and five intervals of 0.9999999 and 1.0000001 (positive,
  negative, straddling with odd and even n). 36 checks.
- `rounded_powers_as_in_both_builds()`: 4000 draws (3625 kept) of n from 1 to
  60 and intervals certain to take the rounded products (a bound 0, tiny,
  huge or infinite, the other bound with a power from about 2^-1000 to 2^1000,
  where the order tells; any interval for n = 1, 2). The reference computes
  the right-to-left binary exponentiation of the magnitudes, each product in
  the rounding direction set for it with `fesetround()` and `rnd_keep()`, and
  composes the bounds by the signs; the bounds must be equal (zero signs not
  compared).

## Behaviour change

- `pow(x, n)`, n >= 3, from the exact products: the lower bound of a positive
  power (upper bound of a negative odd power) is now the tightest or one double
  beyond for every n; it was more than one double below for most n above 2^28,
  up to thousands of doubles near 2^32.
- SSE2 intervals only, where the rounded products are taken (a bound 0 or
  infinite, a power below 2^-968 or beyond the largest double): the bounds are
  now those of the FPU intervals; they change by a few doubles in about half of
  such intervals, with the same accuracy. n = 1, 2 and the FPU intervals are
  unchanged.

## API and ABI

No public API or ABI change. `gaol_core::MSB_position()` (defined without
`static` in `gaol_interval_sse.cpp`, declared in no header) is no longer
compiled, so the symbol leaves the library where visibility is not hidden;
`reverse_bits()` was INLINE.

## Risks

Low. The fix is one line and sound by construction (all quantities rounded
upward and non-negative). The SSE2 code now compiled is GAOL's own older code
(same intrinsics), proven product-for-product equal to the FPU code and
checked against an independent reference in both builds, at -O3 and -O0. No
platform assumption beyond each double operation being rounded once to a
double, which GAOL already requires (x87 refused). What the CI must confirm:
the new checks on ARM, s390x, ppc64le, riscv64, Windows (MSVC, MinGW), macOS,
and that `std::fesetround()` in the test's reference works there as in
`fused_and_cancellative()`.

## Open questions and decisions for the maintainer

1. `configure-clean` fails `rounding_direction` since #57 (width/center of the
   empty set written `[empty]`, the test of #50 expects `empty`): to fix in
   `configure-clean`, not here.
2. A zero bound (`pow([0, b], n)`, `pow([a, 0], n)`) sends both bounds to the
   rounded products: the upper bound is above the tightest for 88 % of b in
   [0.5, 2] (n = 3 to 10), up to 10 doubles. `ipow_exact_dn(0)` could give 0
   exactly (one line). More generally one bound out of range makes both bounds
   take the rounded products. Decide whether to change.
3. The sign of a zero bound still differs between SSE2 and FPU intervals
   (even powers of intervals containing 0 in the rounded products, `sqr()`):
   left, per the decision not to normalise zeros; documented.
4. The guarantee changes from n 2^-104 to 5 n log2(n) 2^-104 (proved bound
   (3s + 2m) n 2^-104, measured 1.7 log2(n) n 2^-104): the old one was exceeded
   from n = 4. Please reread the wording.
5. The old MSB-first SSE2 code (`MSB_position()`, `reverse_bits()`) is kept
   under `#if 0`, as GAOL kept the other order: delete it?
