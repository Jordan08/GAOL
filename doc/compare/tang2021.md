# The benchmark of Tang et al. (2021) on GAOL v5

Part of the [comparison](README.md) of GAOL with other interval libraries
(GAOL v5).

The benchmark of interval libraries of

> Xuan Tang, Zachary Ferguson, Teseo Schneider, Denis Zorin, Shoaib Kamil and
> Daniele Panozzo, *A Cross-Platform Benchmark for Interval Computation
> Libraries*, Parallel Processing and Applied Mathematics (PPAM 2022), Lecture
> Notes in Computer Science 13827, Springer, 2023, pp. 415–427,
> [doi:10.1007/978-3-031-30445-3_35](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-30445-3_35);
> preprint [arXiv:2110.06215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06215), 12 October 2021,

run on GAOL v5. Its code is
[geometryprocessing/intervals](https://github.com/geometryprocessing/intervals)
(MIT license), the repository the paper's
[web page](https://geometryprocessing.github.io/intervals/) links to, taken at
its last commit, `0a41a99c22689892da026b25398ff975f1d741d5` (24 May 2023,
which adds the license to the code of 30 January 2021). Nothing of it is kept
here: [tang2021/fetch.sh](tang2021/fetch.sh) downloads it, and
[tang2021/gaol.patch](tang2021/gaol.patch) adds GAOL to it.

**In short**: on 100 000 inputs of each of the benchmark's 132 expressions,
13 200 000 results in each of the SSE2 and FPU builds of GAOL, every interval
of GAOL encloses the exact value of the expression the program computes, the
eight single operations give the tightest interval every time, and GAOL's
intervals are narrower than those of filib's C version, the paper's reference,
at all but 1 488 inputs, where they are as wide. The paper's own criterion
fails at 9 884 inputs, in six FPBench expressions: each has a decimal constant
that is not a double, which the benchmark's query writes as the decimal while
the program computes with the double. These are a defect of the benchmark, not
of GAOL; no bug of GAOL was found.

## The benchmark

The paper compares filib (its C version), filib++ (its `native_switched`,
`multiplicative` and `pred_succ` modes), Boost.Interval and BIAS on 132
expressions:

- 28 of the authors (`src/methods.hpp`): the four operations, `sqrt`, `exp`,
  `sin` and `cos`; ten arithmetic expressions of 6 to 10 variables
  (`expr1` to `expr10`); ten random expressions of 6 to 9 variables with
  `sqrt`, `exp`, `sin` and `cos` (`extra_function1` to `extra_function10`);
- 104 expressions of [FPBench](https://fpbench.org/)
  (`src/FPBench_methods.hpp`), with `sqrt`, `exp`, `log`, `sin`, `cos`, `tan`
  and `atan`, on the inputs of their preconditions.

Each expression is evaluated on a million inputs drawn at random, each input
variable being the interval of one double. A result is correct when it
contains the exact value of the expression at the input: the benchmark prints
each result as a query `lo <= expression <= hi`, all its numbers written as
exact rationals, which a Mathematica script checks
(`mathmetica_notebooks/verify_queries.wls`). It also measures the widths of
the intervals (`gap_bin`) and their speed (`speed_bin`: 1 000 inputs of each
expression, each evaluated 10 000 times). The paper finds every library correct
on the arithmetic, Boost.Interval wrong on `exp`, `sin` and `cos` (it calls
the C library's functions), BIAS wrong on one random expression, and filib++'s
`native_switched` mode wrong on four FPBench expressions, two of which it
names: `polarToCarthesian, x` and `sineOrder3`. GAOL is cited, among the
libraries that widen their results by a relative factor, but not run.

## GAOL in the benchmark

The benchmark adds a library by giving its type, `lower()` and `upper()` in
`src/interval.hpp` and a line in each of its three tests. `gaol.patch` does
that for GAOL, which CMake finds through its package (`gaol::gaol`, whose flags,
`-frounding-math` among them, then compile the whole benchmark), and changes
what the benchmark needs to run here:

- **GAOL**: `gaol::interval`, its bounds `left()` and `right()`, and a line in
  `RUN_QUERY` (`tests/query/query.cpp`), `RUN_GAP` (`tests/gap/gap.cpp`) and
  `RUN_SPEED` (`tests/speed/speed.cpp`), after the other libraries';
- **the rounding direction**: GAOL leaves the direction upward, for the whole
  program, from its initialization (see
  [The rounding direction](../using.md#the-rounding-direction)). Each line of
  GAOL is followed by `gaol::restore_rounding()`, as GAOL's documentation
  advises a program that needs rounding to nearest: filib's C version, written
  for it, and the benchmark's own doubles (the drawing and the checking of the
  inputs) are computed as in a run without GAOL;
- **Boost** is optional: its lines are left out when CMake does not find it,
  as here (`WITH_BOOST`); filib++ was already optional, taken when
  `/usr/local/lib` holds its library;
- **the inputs**: the repository holds the recorded inputs of the 28 first
  expressions, the paper's, in `rational_datas`; `fetch.sh` takes the first
  `N` of each. FPBench's inputs are not there (the benchmark read them from
  files it does not have, out of an empty vector); they are drawn instead, by the
  benchmark's generator (`std::default_random_engine`), seeded with the name of
  each expression, so that any share of the expressions draws the same ones:
  they are not the paper's;
- **two defects of the benchmark**: `rational_to_string()` never freed its
  buffer (a few gigabytes over a run), and an infinite bound would have stopped
  the program (GMP's `mpq_set_d()` raises `SIGFPE` on an infinity): it is
  written `Infinity`, as Mathematica reads it;
- **for the checker**: the query lines of each expression are preceded by its
  name (`EXPRESSION, sineOrder3`) and those of each input by the input, exactly
  (`INPUT, 0x1.8p+1, ...`), lines Mathematica's script skips; and
  `INTERVALS_SHARD=k/n` runs one share of the expressions, so that `n`
  processes check them in parallel.

## How the results are checked

Mathematica is replaced by [tang2021/check.py](tang2021/check.py), which reads
the queries of GAOL and decides each one exactly. The exact value is computed
with rational arithmetic as long as the expression is rational (62 of the
132 are), then enclosed with MPFR (through gmpy2), each bound rounded in its
direction, at 256 bits, and at 2000 bits where 256 do not decide. A query is:

- **right** when the exact value is in GAOL's interval;
- **wrong** when it is not (an empty result included);
- **undefined** when the exact expression has no real value at the input (a
  square root or a logarithm of a negative number, a division by zero), which
  Mathematica's script does not count as wrong either;
- **undecided** when 2000 bits do not decide (none was).

Each query is checked twice:

- **(a) as the benchmark prints it**, its decimal constants being exact
  decimals: `0.954929658551372` in `sineOrder3` is 954929658551372/10¹⁵. This
  is the paper's criterion;
- **(b) as the program computes it**: the expression of the C++ source, on the
  inputs of the line `INPUT`, its constants being the doubles the program
  computes, the literals rounded to nearest when compiling. The operations
  between constants, `(3.14159265359) / 180.0` in `polarToCarthesian`, are
  computed when the program runs (`-frounding-math` keeps them from being
  folded), where the compiler puts them: GCC does not model the rounding
  direction (GCC bug 34678), and the quotient may be computed to nearest or
  upward. Both are tried, and how many queries hold with one only is counted.
  This is what GAOL was asked to enclose.

For the eight single operations, the script checks that GAOL's interval is the
tightest, [RD(v), RU(v)], v being the exact value; and for every input, it
compares the width of GAOL's interval with that of filib's C version, the
reference of the paper's sizes.

`check.py selftest` checks the script against mpmath at 2000 bits (the seven
functions at 14 000 points, on 3 500 narrow intervals, and on 300 intervals
around the extrema of sin and cos and the poles of tan) and against
`fractions` (2 000 rational expressions), and its decisions on queries
written by hand.

## Results

GAOL v5 built with CMake in Release by GCC 9.4, on Linux x86-64 (Intel
i7-1185G7), and the benchmark compiled with its own flags (`-O3
-march=native`) and GAOL's; 100 000 inputs of each expression, the 132
expressions in all:

| | SSE2 build (`GAOL_SIMD=ON`) | FPU build (`GAOL_SIMD=OFF`) |
|---|---|---|
| Results checked | 13 200 000 | 13 200 000 |
| (b) wrong: GAOL's interval misses the value of what the program computes | 0 | 0 |
| (a) wrong: the paper's query does not hold | 9 884 | 9 884 |
| Undefined, undecided, empty or unbounded | 0 | 0 |
| Single operations (+, −, ×, ÷, sqrt, exp, sin, cos) with the tightest interval | 800 000 of 800 000 | 800 000 of 800 000 |
| Narrower than filib's C version / as wide / wider | 13 198 512 / 1 488 / 0 | 13 198 512 / 1 488 / 0 |

The two builds give the same tables, line for line. GAOL and the benchmark
built by Clang 18 (SSE2, 10 000 inputs of each expression) give the same
classes: no wrong result under (b), and 991 under (a), in the same six
expressions.

### The failures of the paper's criterion: a defect of the benchmark

The 9 884 queries that do not hold as the benchmark prints them are in six
FPBench expressions, each of which has a decimal constant that is not a
double:

| Expression | Queries that do not hold, of 100 000 | Its constants that are not doubles |
|---|---|---|
| `polarToCarthesian_x` | 4 446 | 3.14159265359, divided by 180 |
| `polarToCarthesian_y` | 4 772 | 3.14159265359, divided by 180 |
| `verhulst` | 374 | 1.11 |
| `sineOrder3` | 199 | 0.954929658551372, 0.12900613773279798 |
| `hartman3` | 71 | 0.3689, 0.117, 0.2673 and others |
| `instantaneousCurrent` | 22 | 3.14159265359 |

The C++ expression computes with the double nearest each constant, and GAOL
encloses, tightly, the expression on these doubles; the query writes the
decimal, whose value can lie just outside. Each of the 9 884 holds as the
program computes it, under (b): they are not wrong results of GAOL, but
queries that do not describe the expression evaluated (*not applicable*). The
other seven expressions with such constants (`carbonGas`,
`carthesianToPolar_theta`, `doppler1` to `doppler3`, `hartman6`,
`predatorPrey`) had no failure: the difference stayed inside GAOL's
intervals. The two failures the paper names for filib++'s `native_switched`
mode, `polarToCarthesian, x` and `sineOrder3`, are in this list, and that
mode, as GAOL, computes the arithmetic with the rounding direction of the
processor, which gives tight intervals: the same defect may explain them
(not checked here, filib++ not being run). In `polarToCarthesian`, the
quotient 3.14159265359 / 180.0 was computed to nearest: every query holds with
it, while 66 209 (`x`) and 59 433 (`y`) would not with the quotient rounded
upward.

`verhulst`, 4x / (1 + x / 1.11), at one of the benchmark's inputs:

```cpp
#include <cmath>
#include <cstdio>
#include <gaol/gaol.h>

int main()
{
  const double x = std::ldexp(0x1e305fc67421cd, -56);   // 0x1.e305fc67421cdp-4
  gaol::interval X(x);
  gaol::interval r = (4.0 * X) / (1.0 + X / 1.11);
  std::printf("[%a, %a]\n", r.left(), r.right());   // [0x1.b4a2b1e47dfe6p-2, 0x1.b4a2b1e47dfe8p-2]
  gaol::cleanup();
  return 0;
}
```

The exact value of 4x / (1 + x / c) is in this interval for c the double
nearest 1.11, and not for c = 111/100, as Python's `fractions` show.

### The sizes of the intervals

GAOL's interval is narrower than that of filib's C version at 13 198 512 of
the 13 200 000 inputs and as wide at 1 488 (in `i4`, `sphere`,
`NMSE_section_3_5`, `test06_sums4_sum1`, `rigidBody2` and `rigidBody1`); it is
never wider. The median ratio of the widths of the two is 0.5 for the four
operations and `sqrt`, 0.09 for `exp`, 0.05 and 0.04 for `sin` and `cos` on
[0, π/4], 0.33 for the median expression, and about 10⁻⁹ in the three FPBench
expressions that take the sine, the cosine or the tangent of arguments up to
1.6·10⁹ (`NMSE_example_3_3`, `NMSE_problem_3_3_2`, `NMSE_problem_3_3_5`),
where filib's C bounds are a billion times wider than GAOL's, which are the
tightest. `work/results/<variant>/results.md` gives the table of the 132
expressions.

### The speed (indicative)

The speed test, 1 000 inputs of each expression each evaluated 10 000 times, of
GAOL and filib's C version, both built with Clang 18 (with which GAOL v5's
timings are made) in the SSE2 build, on the i7-1185G7 while it was busy with
other work (a load of about 3.5): the times are indicative only.

- GAOL takes less time than filib's C version on 95 of the 132 expressions:
  0.35 to 0.75 times its time on the four operations, 0.41 to 0.62 on the ten
  arithmetic expressions, 0.33 to 2.35 on FPBench's (less on 80 of the 104);
  0.53 for the median expression, and 268.5 s in all against 344.9 s.
- It takes more on the elementary functions: 1.43 times on `exp`, 1.64 on
  `sqrt`, 1.87 on `sin` and 3.89 on `cos`, whose inputs the speed test draws
  among ±(2³¹ − 1), not in [0, π/4] as the other tests (GAOL's bounds there
  are the tightest, and filib's far wider on large arguments, as above); 0.99
  to 1.68 on the ten random expressions, and up to 2.35 on the FPBench
  expressions of sines, cosines and tangents of large arguments.

### What differs from the paper's runs

- 100 000 inputs of each expression, not 1 000 000: the script checks a few
  thousand queries a second in each process (`N=1000000` runs the million);
- FPBench's inputs are drawn, not the paper's, which are not published; those
  of the 28 first expressions are the paper's;
- Mathematica is replaced by `check.py`, which also checks the expression as
  the program computes it;
- only GAOL and filib's C version are run: Boost.Interval (which
  `doc/compare/code` compares otherwise), filib++ and BIAS are not installed
  here; the benchmark takes the first two when CMake finds them;
- one machine, Linux x86-64, where the paper ran on four platforms;
- the README of the benchmark counts 134 expressions, the paper 132, which
  is what the benchmark has.

## Running it again

```bash
cd doc/compare/tang2021
./run.sh                                    # fetch.sh, then the SSE2 and FPU builds of GAOL
N=1000000 JOBS=4 ./run.sh                   # the paper's million inputs
VARIANTS=sse SUFFIX=-clang CC=clang-18 CXX=clang++-18 SPEED=1 ./run.sh   # with the speed test
```

It needs git, curl, CMake, a C++ compiler, Python 3 with gmpy2 (and mpmath
for `check.py selftest`), and the network for `fetch.sh` only. `fetch.sh`
clones the benchmark at its commit without its 1.5 GB of recorded inputs,
downloads the first `N` inputs of each of the 28 files it needs, decompressing
them as they come, applies `gaol.patch`, and builds GMP 6.3.0 under
`work/prefix` when the system has no `gmp.h`; configuring the benchmark
downloads filib's C version and CLI11, at the commits its CMake files give.
`run.sh` builds GAOL from this repository (CMake, Release) for each variant,
builds the benchmark with it, runs the queries in `JOBS` processes, each
checking its share with `check.py`, and writes the tables of
`work/results/<variant>/results.md`. Its variables:

| Variable | Default | |
|---|---|---|
| `N` | `100000` | Inputs of each expression (the paper's: 1 000 000) |
| `JOBS` | `2` | Jobs of the builds, and processes running and checking the queries |
| `VARIANTS` | `sse fpu` | The builds of GAOL: `GAOL_SIMD` ON and OFF |
| `SPEED` | `0` | `1` runs the speed test too, into `work/results/<variant>/speed.md` |
| `CC`, `CXX` | `gcc`, `g++` | The compilers of GAOL and of the benchmark |
| `SUFFIX` | | Added to the names of the variants (`-clang`: `work/gaol-sse-clang`, `work/results/sse-clang`...), so that builds by other compilers stay apart |
| `FORCE_GAOL` | | `1` builds GAOL again, after a change |
| `WORK`, `PREFIX` | `work`, `work/prefix` | Where everything is downloaded and built, and where GMP is installed |
| `CMAKE`, `PYTHON` | `cmake`, `python3` | |

The benchmark is not run by the continuous integration: it needs the network,
and the checks of 100 000 inputs of each expression take about
half an hour for each build, in two processes, on an Intel i7-1185G7.
