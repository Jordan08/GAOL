# ITF1788 on GAOL v5

Part of the [comparison](README.md) of GAOL with other libraries.

[ITF1788](https://github.com/oheim/ITF1788), the Interval Test Framework for
IEEE 1788 of Marco Nehmeier, Maximilian Kiesner and Oliver Heimlich, holds
test cases of the operations of IEEE 1788-2015: those of libieeep1788 (Marco
Nehmeier), of C-XSC, of FI_LIB and of MPFI, which Oliver Heimlich converted,
and his own for `atan2`, `absRev`, `powRev1`, `powRev2`, the constructors and
the exceptions. They are written in a format of its own, one case per line of
its `.itl` files:

    add [1.0,2.0] [3.0,4.0] = [4.0,6.0];
    absRevBin [1.0, 1.0] [entire] = [-1.0, 1.0];

ITF1788 converts them into test programs for several libraries; its plugin
for GAOL, written for GAOL 4, calls GAOL by GAOL's own names and leaves out
most of the operations GAOL v5 adds. Here the whole suite runs on GAOL v5
through the names of the standard, `gaol_ieee1788` (see
[Using GAOL](../using.md#the-names-of-ieee-1788-2015)), in the SSE2 and in the
FPU builds.

## In short

- **9542 cases**, in the 19 files of ITF1788. **7285 pass**: GAOL v5 gives the
  result the case expects, the tightest one, the 1347 cases of `pow`, the 163
  of `pown`, the 172 of `mulRevToPair` without decorations and 802 of the 804
  cases of `powRev1` and `powRev2` included.
- **No bug of GAOL v5 was found**. The **10 failures** are of two kinds:
  - **2 results that are valid but not the tightest**, from `pownRev` at the
    largest double: 1 double beyond the tightest bound, accurate in the sense
    of IEEE 1788-2015 (12.10.1), which requires of this operation a valid
    result and recommends an accurate one (12.10.2). Its accuracy is the one
    [Accuracy of the operations](../accuracy.md) gives. The 15 cases of
    `pown` with a negative exponent that were 1 or 2 doubles beyond the
    tightest bound give it since the integer powers compute x<sup>−n</sup>
    from exact products (point B of `TODO.md`).
  - **8 cases where ITF1788 expects a result that is not the tightest**, from
    the reverse functions `sinRev`, `cosRev` and `tanRev` of libieeep1788's
    tests (6) and `powRev2` of Oliver Heimlich's (2): GAOL v5's result is
    narrower than the expected one and encloses the exact result, which
    mpmath computes; it is the tightest one in 7 of them.
- **560 cases are not provided**: eight trigonometric functions of FI_LIB and
  MPFI that are not in the standard (`cot`, `sec`, `csc`, their hyperbolic
  versions, `acot` and `acoth`: 403), `pownRev` for an exponent p ≤ 0 (94),
  `overlap` (48) and the reduction operations (15). `gaol/gaol_ieee1788.h`
  lists those of the standard.
- **1687 cases are not applicable**: they check the decorated intervals and
  NaI (Clause 11), which GAOL v5 does not have.
- The SSE2 and the FPU builds give the same result in every case.

The deliberate differences of GAOL v5 from the standard
([What differs from GAOL](../differences.md)) do not show here: the hybrid
`pow`, which takes `pown` for an integer exponent, is `gaol::pow`, while
`gaol_ieee1788::pow` is the `pow` of the standard; and the constructors of
intervals with an infinite bound, which follow IBEX (`interval(+∞)` is empty),
give the empty set where the cases of ITF1788 expect it,
`numsToInterval(+∞, +∞)` and `numsToInterval(−∞, −∞)`.

## How the cases are run

`itf1788/itf1788.py generate` reads the `.itl` files itself, rather than
through ITF1788's converter, which needs PLY and Boost.Test, and writes a C++
program per file. Each case is one call of the operation by the name
`gaol_ieee1788` gives it, `using namespace gaol_ieee1788;` being the program's
only namespace directive, its result compared with the one the case gives,
and a line written per case, which `itf1788.py report` counts and classifies.
Each program ends with `gaol::cleanup()`.

- **Literals.** A number of a case is the double nearest to it, as in the
  plugins of ITF1788, where it is a double literal of C++ or of Octave:
  `[-2.0, -0.1]` is the interval whose lower bound is the double nearest to
  −0.1, not the interval enclosing −0.1, and the results the cases expect are
  computed from these doubles. The generator computes each double
  (`float.fromhex`, `float` in Python), writes it as the decimal literal that
  converts back to it, and builds the intervals with the numeric constructor,
  `interval(l, u)`. Only the strings of `b-textToInterval` are read, by
  `gaol_ieee1788::textToInterval`, the reader of the literals of the standard.
- **Names.** ITF1788 names the reverse functions with their optional argument
  `absRevBin`, `sinRevBin`..., `mulRevTen`, and `pownRevBin` (c, x, p): they
  are `absRev(c, x)`, `sinRev(c, x)`, `mulRev(b, c, x)` and `pownRev(c, x, p)`.
  Two operations of the cases are not in IEEE 1788-2015: `pos(x)`, which
  libieeep1788's cases use, is GAOL's unary `+x`, and `cbrt`, which MPFI's
  cases use, is `rootn(x, 3)`.
- **Comparison.** As in ITF1788, the result must be the tightest one the case
  gives. An interval is a set: a zero bound equals a zero of either sign. A
  number must have the sign of zero that 12.12.8 gives, −0 for `inf` and +0
  for the other numeric functions, where its exact value is 0; the midpoint of
  an interval whose exact midpoint is not 0 but rounds to it, such as
  [−2<sup>−1073</sup>, 2<sup>−1074</sup>], may be −0 or +0: IEEE 754 rounds
  −2<sup>−1075</sup> to −0, which GAOL v5 returns, and the case writes 0.0. A
  NaN equals a NaN.
- **Exceptions.** 33 cases expect an exception of IEEE 1788
  (`UndefinedOperation`, `PossiblyUndefinedOperation`) besides their result:
  the result is checked; GAOL v5 signals none of these exceptions.
- **Cases not run.** A case of a decorated operation (`d-textToInterval`,
  `setDec`, `isNaI`...), or with a decorated interval or NaI among its inputs
  or results, is not applicable. A case of an operation GAOL v5 does not
  provide is counted as not provided, and so is a call of `pownRev` with
  p ≤ 0, which throws `std::invalid_argument` rather than give an interval it
  has not computed.
- **Failures.** `itf1788.py report` classifies each failure. A result that
  encloses the expected one is valid; it is a bug where the standard requires
  the tightest result (12.10.2: the basic operations, the integer and absmax
  functions; the cancellative operations, the set operations and the
  constructors), and conforming elsewhere, the report then proving it
  accurate where it can: within one double of the tightest, or, for `pow` and
  `pown`, within one double outside of the hull of the tightest result and of
  the values, computed with mpmath, at the corners of the box of the inputs
  moved one double outward. A result that misses part of the expected one is
  compared with the exact result, computed with mpmath at 2000 bits for the
  reverse functions of sin, cos and tan (the least and the greatest points of
  the solution set among the bounds of x, the preimages of the bounds of c and
  the poles) and of pow (the hull of the solutions that
  `tests/pow_rel_values.py` computes for GAOL's own tests, by cells between
  the points c<sup>1/y</sup>, log(c)/log(x), 0 and the bounds of x): it is a
  bug if it misses part of the exact result, and a defect of the case
  otherwise. The reference of sin, cos and tan gives the result ITF1788
  expects in the 40 other cases of these functions with a bounded x. A wrong
  number, sign of zero or boolean, an exception, and two pieces of
  `mulRevToPair` in the wrong order are bugs.

## The failures

### Valid, not the tightest: `pownRev`

Both enclose the tightest result, and are accurate (12.10.1), which
IEEE 1788-2015 recommends for this operation without requiring it
(12.10.2).

**`pow(x, y)`** gives the tightest result in its 1347 cases. 68 of them, with
an infinite bound or a base from 0 and an exponent that is not above 0, were
1 to 8 doubles beyond it while GAOL v5 computed exp(y·log(x)) there:
`pow([0.1, 0.5], [−∞, 2.5])` was [0x1.9e7c6e43390afp−9, +∞], 8 doubles below
0.1<sup>2.5</sup>. It now takes CORE-MATH's `pow` at the corners of every box,
and the limits of x<sup>y</sup>, 0 or +∞, at the corners where the base is 0
or +∞ or the exponent infinite (point B of `TODO.md`).

**`pown(x, n)`** gives the tightest result in its 163 cases without
decorations. The 15 with
n = −2, −3, −7 and −8 that were 1 or 2 doubles beyond it, while GAOL v5
computed 1/x<sup>n</sup>, one rounded division more than x<sup>n</sup>
(`pown([13.1], −3)` was [0x1.d26df4d8b1831p−12, 0x1.d26df4d8b1834p−12], the
tightest upper bound being 0x1.d26df4d8b1832p−12), now take the inverse of
the exact products of x<sup>n</sup>, corrected and rounded once (point B of
`TODO.md`).

**`pownRev(c, 7)` at ±MAX**, 2 cases: the seventh root of the largest double
is 1 double beyond the tightest, as [Accuracy](../accuracy.md) says of the
roots of `nth_root()`.

### ITF1788 expects a result that is not the tightest: `sinRev`, `cosRev`, `tanRev`, `powRev2`

Six of these cases come from libieeep1788's tests of the reverse functions, whose
results the standard requires to be valid only, and they expect a bound one
double, once two, beyond the tightest one. GAOL v5's result is narrower and
encloses the exact solution set, which mpmath gives: it is the tightest result
in five cases, and one double beyond it, still one double inside the expected
one, in the sixth. In
`cosRevBin [-1.0, -1.0] [3.14, 3.15] = [0x1.921fb54442d18p+1, 0x1.921fb54442d1ap+1]`,
the solution set is {π}, whose tightest enclosure is
[0x1.921fb54442d18p+1, 0x1.921fb54442d19p+1], GAOL v5's result.

The other two come from Oliver Heimlich's tests of `powRev2`, the y with
a<sup>y</sup> in c: `powRev2 [0.25, 0.5] [2.0, infinity] [entire]` expects
[−∞, +∞], and `powRev2 [0.25, 1.0] [2.0, infinity] [entire]` [−∞, 0]. For a
in [0.25, 1), a<sup>y</sup> ≥ 2 holds for y ≤ log(2)/log(a), at most −0.5
(a = 0.25), and 1<sup>y</sup> = 1 never: the solutions are [−∞, −0.5], the
result of GAOL v5 in both cases, and the tightest one.

## What the suite does not test

ITF1788 has no case of `exp2m1`, `exp10m1`, `log2p1`, `log10p1`, `rSqrt`,
`sinPi`, `cosPi`, `tanPi`, `asinPi`, `acosPi`, `atanPi`, `atan2Pi` (Table 10.5),
`atan2Rev1` and `atan2Rev2` (Table 10.1), `intervalToText` or
`intervalToExact` (Clause 13; `exactToInterval` is `textToInterval`, which it
tests); its `rootn` has 3 cases and its `hypot` 17. GAOL's own tests check
these functions ([Tests](../tests.md)).

## Running it again

The suite is not part of the tests of the builds nor of the continuous
integration: it needs the network, to fetch ITF1788, and runs for a few
minutes (2 minutes and a half with two jobs on an Intel i7-1185G7, both
builds of GAOL v5 included). It needs git, CMake, a C++ compiler,
`pkg-config` and Python 3 with mpmath:

```bash
doc/compare/itf1788/run.sh   # fetches ITF1788, builds GAOL v5 twice, runs, writes the tables below
FORCE_GAOL=1 doc/compare/itf1788/run.sh   # the same after a change of GAOL v5, which it builds again
# with another compiler, in a WORK of its own, where GAOL v5 is built with it
CC=clang-18 CXX=clang++-18 WORK=doc/compare/itf1788/work/clang REPORT=/tmp/itf1788-clang.md doc/compare/itf1788/run.sh
```

The tables below were made with Clang 21.1; GCC 15.2 and Clang 18 gave the
same results before the integer powers changed (point B of `TODO.md`).

`fetch.sh` clones ITF1788 at its last commit, `b6ee1e2` (23 September 2018),
into `WORK` (`doc/compare/itf1788/work`, which git ignores); ITF1788 is not
copied into GAOL's repository. `run.sh` builds and installs GAOL v5 there with
CMake, in Release, with the SSE2 intervals and with the FPU ones
(`-DGAOL_SIMD=OFF`), generates the programs, compiles them with the flags of
`gaol.pc` and `-O2`, runs them, and rewrites the tables between the markers
below; the text around them, which comments on the results, has to be checked
by hand against the new tables. Its variables are described at its top:
`WORK`, `CC`, `CXX`, `CMAKE`, `JOBS`, `BUILDS`, `CXXFLAGS_ITF`, `NOT_PROVIDED`
(operations of `gaol_ieee1788` to count as not provided rather than run),
`FORCE_GAOL` (rebuild GAOL v5 after a change) and `REPORT`.

| File | |
|---|---|
| `itf1788/fetch.sh` | Fetches ITF1788 at the commit the report was made with |
| `itf1788/itf1788.py` | Reads the `.itl` files and writes the programs (`generate`), and writes the tables of this report from what the programs printed (`report`) |
| `itf1788/itf1788_gaol.h` | The comparison and the output of the programs |
| `itf1788/run.sh` | Fetches, builds, generates, compiles, runs and reports |
